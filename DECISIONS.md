# Decision record: audiogen

Decisions for this repo, current state only. Each entry has the date, the context and the decision. Check the
existing entries for a conflict before adding one, and rewrite an entry when AJ changes it.

### Two LLM backends: the Claude subscription first, the API as the fallback
**Date:** 2026-10-16
**Context:** AJ asked for both at the start.
**Decision:** The character pass, the casting pass and the screenplay run through headless Claude Code under AJ's login by default (`--backend auto`). On a usage-limit message they fall back to the Anthropic API, wait for the reset, or fail, per `--on-limit`. Every chapter records which backend ran. On the cluster the API fallback can't run, because `/usr/bin/python3` on dev-arch doesn't have the `anthropic` package (checked 2026-11-09), so a pass that hits the limit with `--on-limit api`, the app's default, fails. AJ said on 2026-11-09 to leave that until it causes a problem.

### Breeze TTS 2 through breeze-server is the default renderer
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Context:** After a side-by-side test on chapter 6 of The Hundred Reigns, AJ said "I like breeze a bit better than drama". After hearing the Breeze-TTS-2.cpp Vulkan build, he said "Vulkan directed sounds great. Let's make that the default". On the laptop it renders at about 2x realtime, against about 0.25x for Drama 3.
**Decision:** `render` uses Breeze with the `cpp` runner unless `serial.yaml` names another engine or runner. A line with a direction uses Voice Direction, and every other line uses a plain clone of the speaker's cast clip. AJ heard little difference between the two, with a slight preference for directed. Drama 3 stays available with `--engine drama3`. Chapters 1 to 6 of The Hundred Reigns are Drama 3 renders.

### breeze-server is built from source
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Context:** Windows Defender flagged the Breeze-TTS-2.cpp v0.1.6 Windows release zip as Trojan:Win32/Malgent. The maintainer uploads that zip by hand, with no CI build and no attestation. A review of the source rated it low risk.
**Decision:** Every breeze-server is built from tag v0.1.6 (commit 87b43667) with its pinned submodules: ggml 36da5713 and shine ab5e3526. The Mac's build adds one upstream ggml commit, `deploy/macos/ggml-metal-left-pad.patch` (`docs/machines.md`).

### Every render writes Ogg Opus
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Context:** AJ: "this should always output .opus files instead of .flac files".
**Decision:** Both engines write `audio/chNNNN.opus`. Breeze audio stays at 24 kHz, and Drama 3 audio is resampled to 48 kHz, because Opus takes only a few sample rates. The Breeze join asks for 40 kbps, and a Drama 3 render leaves the bitrate to the encoder.

### Every Breeze render checks its takes
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Context:** Breeze sometimes loops, repeating a word, or drops the end of a line. The take-length guard catches only the gross cases. AJ asked for the transcript check to be part of the process.
**Decision:** After the join, `render` transcribes each new take and aligns it with the script line. A take with three or more inserted or dropped words is flagged. The result goes to `take_check.json` and `render.check`, and the chapter page lists the takes to listen to. When no recognizer is available, the check is recorded as skipped. It doesn't fail the render.

### A render takes a flagged take again before the join
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Context:** The take check ran after the join and only listed the flagged takes for AJ to listen to, and he listens while driving. In the 34 checked chapters of The Hundred Reigns, 17 of 7,602 takes were flagged, about one in every two chapters. Claude proposed taking them again, and AJ said "good idea".
**Decision:** After the takes and before the join, a Breeze render under breeze-server transcribes its new takes with Parakeet while the server is still up. It takes a flagged line again with new seeds, in up to two rounds, and keeps a new take only when its transcript is closer to the line. A take from an earlier render is taken again only when an earlier check flagged it (the next entry). With Qwen3-ASR as the recognizer nothing is taken again, because Qwen3-ASR needs the card that breeze-server holds. A saved take records a hash of its audio, and the check reuses a transcript only for the same audio, because a take that is made again keeps its key. In a scratch copy on the laptop on 2026-11-11, the eight flagged takes of chapters 16, 19, 24, 27, 36 and 37 came out flagged again from their seeds, and the first new take replaced each one. Two of the eight were lines of 58 and 64 words that had lost 25 and 30 of them.

### A render takes a saved take again when a check flagged it
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** AJ heard a garbled narrator line in chapter 33 of The Hundred Reigns at 8:55. The take check had flagged it, but the chapter was rendered before the retake before the join reached containers, and a render checked only the takes it made, so no later render would replace it. Ten flagged takes sat in nine rendered chapters. AJ said yes to a way to take them again.
**Decision:** Under breeze-server with Parakeet, a render puts a saved take through the check before the join when `take_check.json` holds a flagged verdict for that take and that audio. Parakeet hears the saved take again, and when it is still flagged the line is taken again with new seeds, and a new take replaces the saved one only when it is closer to the line. The saved take is not made again from its own seed first, because on the other node or without bit-exact seeds the remake would differ and could replace a better take unseen. Every line taken again records its last round as `retake_rounds`, and a line that has been through every round is left alone, because the same seeds would give the same takes. A join-only run changes nothing, and neither does a machine whose Parakeet cannot run. In a scratch copy on the laptop on 2026-11-12, with its own copies of the takes, a render of chapter 33 made no line again, heard the saved take of line 72 as flagged, and kept the first new take, whose transcript matched the line but for the name Eole. A second render took nothing. After the deploy on 2026-11-12 the nine chapters were rendered one at a time on an empty queue, about a minute each on the laptop. Nine flagged lines got a new take in the first round, and line 62 of chapter 30 was made new with 14 other lines whose takes were out of date. The library then held no flagged take in 9,383.

### A render keeps the chapter's seed
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** The app queued a render with seed 1 unless the request named one, and the scheduler counted a render's reuse with seed 1 too. Chapters 13, 16 and 22 of The Hundred Reigns were rendered with seeds 3, 2 and 2, so a render from the chapter page would have made every one of their takes again. This is package S1 of `docs/take-quality/phase-1.md`.
**Decision:** A render that doesn't name a seed uses the seed in the chapter's `render` record when the last render was Breeze, and 1 otherwise. `breeze.render_seed` decides it for the render and for the scheduler's estimate. The app passes `--seed` to the CLI only when the request names one. The seed box of the Process dialog is empty by default, and the batch an import queues doesn't name a seed. A Drama 3 render still uses 1. In a scratch copy on 2026-11-12, chapters 13, 16 and 22 counted 229, 223 and 166 reusable takes of as many without a seed, and none with seed 1.

### A take has one evidence record, and one function judges it
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** The check kept a take's transcript and its word counts, and threw away the recognizer's token times and everything the join measured. The checks AJ asked for on 2026-11-12 (silences, level, endings, names) all need that evidence, at the one point where a finding costs a single retake: before the join, while breeze-server is up. This is package F1 of `docs/take-quality/phase-1.md`, and the shared contract in `docs/take-quality/README.md` sets the form.
**Decision:** `take_check.json` keeps one entry per take with two versioned blocks. `asr` has the transcript and Parakeet's raw tokens with their start times, durations and log-probabilities. `signal` has what `take_measure.py` reads from the audio at its own sample rate. `takecheck.judge()` derives the word counts, the verdict, the reasons and the repairs from the blocks, and it uses the standard library only. The verdicts and the rule for a flag are the ones from 2026-11-07, so this change by itself doesn't flag a take that the old check passed. A saved entry is reused only when both blocks have the current version, so each of the 9,383 takes checked before this is heard again on its chapter's next render or check. A render hears those saved takes before the join, with the new takes, so a finding in one of them is taken again in the same render. That costs about two minutes of Parakeet on the laptop for a chapter of 190 takes, once per chapter. The rest of this entry is Claude's choice, and AJ hasn't confirmed it.

* A frame of 10 ms is speech when it is within 30 dB of the take's own K-weighted level. Over 626 takes of The Hundred Reigns the frame levels have two peaks, speech near the take's level and pauses about 48 dB below it, and the fewest frames lie 25 to 35 dB below. A silent run is 0.15 s or more without a speech frame, inside the take.
* The level is K-weighted over the speech frames, and the scale is set at each sample rate so that a full-scale 997 Hz sine reads -3.01 LKFS. Without that, the filter made for 24 kHz read 0.03 dB high.
* The names in a line's token map are the words of the bible's pronunciation terms and the capitalized words of each character's name. An alias is not a name, because the aliases include "Father", "Boss" and "the bastard".
* The spread and the largest step of loudness inside a take are measured between windows of one second, over the speech frames only. Over 626 takes, windows of 0.4 s gave a median spread of 10 dB and a median largest step of 15 dB, so ordinary speech filled the range. Windows of one second give a median of 6 dB for both, and 99th percentiles of 11 and 12 dB.
* A render takes lines again at most 8 times, or for an eighth of the chapter's takes when that is more, the worst lines first. A line goes on from the round its take reached in an earlier render, so no render asks for a seed a second time. A voice finding needs four flagged takes of the voice and over a quarter of its takes, for one reason. A take a voice finding covers records `retake_held`, and a later render leaves it alone. The numbers are starting points, to be set again when tracks A1 and A3 add their reasons.
* `take_check.json` has one line per take. Chapter 33 is 302 KB that way and 63 KB in a bundle's zip, against 723 KB with every token on a line of its own.

No take key changed. `VoiceTextTest` pins the keys of a fixed script, and on 2026-11-12 every planned key of the 10,724 lines in 48 chapters of The Hundred Reigns was the same before and after. A render would reuse 9,160 of the 9,383 saved takes before and after. The other 223 are lines of voices that were recast or cast after their chapters were rendered, in chapters 7 to 10, 15 and 18. In a scratch copy on the laptop, a render of chapter 33 without its take of line 72 heard the 188 saved takes before the join, made line 72 again from its seed, flagged it and kept the first new take, which is the take the library holds. Every entry then had both blocks, and a second check heard none of them. On 40 takes of chapter 38 the Mac and the laptop measured the same `signal` block for every take, and Parakeet gave the same transcript for 35. The other five differ in how a name is spelled, so a take's word counts can depend on the node that heard it.

### The take check flags on words, endings, short lines and the signal, and cuts long pauses
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** AJ asked on 2026-11-12 for checks on silences, mispronounced words, names said different ways and odd volume, and he can't review a flagged take, so each check has to end in a retake or a repair. These are packages A1 to A3 of `docs/take-quality/phase-1.md`. Every number here was set from the 9,383 takes of chapters 7 to 48 of The Hundred Reigns, heard by Parakeet on the laptop in a scratch copy on 2026-11-12. Under the rules of 2026-11-07 those takes had 216 suspect and 1 flagged.
**Decision:** `takecheck.judge()` flags a take for the reasons listed in `README.md`, Pipeline per chapter, step 5, and a render takes a flagged line again within the three limits. Over the same takes the rules flag 66 (short 24, swing 21, fading 14, ending 5, level 3, and 1 each for clipped, dropped, rate and substituted), 4 in a chapter at most and 1.6 on average, and leave 22 suspect. No voice comes near a voice finding. The rest of this entry is Claude's choice, and AJ hasn't confirmed it.

* A name in the line matches the recognizer's spelling of it when the spelling is within 0.75 of the name's, by edit distance over the longer of the two, in at most two heard words. Of the name substitutions in the first 1,539 takes, 90% were within 0.57 and the legitimate ones ran to 0.75 ("Eole" heard as "yo"). This turned about 190 suspect takes into passes. The entry records the spellings: Eole was heard 102 ways in 340 takes, Vouivre 88 ways in 158, Casval 33 ways in 247. The cost is that a name also stands for an unrelated short word in its place, so "Thalas" heard as "the" passes. A pronunciation term of one word is a name with its first letter in either case, and a word of a longer term only as the term writes it, so "Your Grace" is not the "grace" of "coup de grace".
* A line of one to three words is flagged for an added or a missing word, and a changed word only makes it suspect. The plan said to require an exact match. The changed words in short lines were the recognizer's homophones: "Simon guessed" came back as "Simon Guest" in five takes, with "read" as "Reed" and "Saber" as "Sabre". The added words were the voice's: "Ah, sure." for "Sure.", "Leonard replied. You." for "Leonard replied.".
* The alignment prefers the substitution between the more similar words, two words match one heard word that is their spellings run together or within 0.25 of it, a contraction matches its two words, and the fragment of a stutter is not a word. Each of these removed false flags that the first run showed.
* A pause is cut only when it is 0.6 s or longer, lies between two words of the line that were heard right, and has no punctuation in the script between them. It is cut to 0.25 s. Breeze pauses without punctuation about as readers do: of 7,031 silent runs placed between two words, 1,542 had no punctuation, with a median of 0.30 s and a 99th percentile of 0.70 s, against medians of 0.41 s at commas and 0.52 s at full stops. So only the longest are cut: 93 in 89 takes, 38 s in 42 chapters. The join applies the cuts in package B1.
* The signal thresholds sit near the 99.8th percentile of each measure, so each costs a handful of retakes a book: an ending 16 dB down (14 takes), a spread of 13.5 dB or a step of 16 dB between windows of one second (21 takes). The rate rule, 0.45 to 2.6 times the voice's median, flags 1 take, and it is there for a take that stops early. The level rule allows 12 dB from the voice's median after the direction's offset, because the takes furthest from their voice were muttered, whispered and shouted lines, and it flags 3.
* A direction's class comes from its words (`breeze.direction_class`), and the offsets are the plan's starting values: whisper -6 dB, soft -3, raised +2, shout +3. AJ sets them when he hears the clips of package B1.
* `take_check.json` records the version of the rules. A render judges the saved evidence again, without hearing it, when the rules are newer, so a new rule reaches a saved take on its chapter's next render. The chapter page and the `backlog` list judge again the same way. The scheduler's estimate of a render reads the stored verdicts, because judging a chapter again took about 1.2 s on the laptop and the estimate runs under the scheduler's lock.
* A vocal event in parentheses is not a word of the line, so a take that ends in "(laugh)" is not missing its ending. A line of one to three words that the recognizer hears nothing in is judged on its length alone, and its dropped words don't flag it.

In a scratch copy on the laptop, a render of chapter 33 judged its 189 saved takes again, took lines 39 and 41 again and kept the new take of both. Line 39 had come out as "Ah. Ah. I emancipated her." and its new take says the line. The chapter then had no flagged take and 1 suspect, against 4 before. Its one cut is a pause of 0.61 s between "corpse" and "at" in line 3.

### The take check's other models are Silero VAD v5, TitaNet small, Moonshine base and ZIPA small
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** Phase 2 of the take quality plan needs a speech detector, a speaker embedding, a second recognizer from another family than Parakeet and a phoneme recognizer, all on the CPU of both breeze nodes (package P0 of `docs/take-quality/phase-2.md`). None had a published speed on this hardware. The spike ran in scratch folders on both nodes on 2026-11-12, over 434 saved takes of The Hundred Reigns (24 takes of each of its 14 busiest voices, 40 lines of one to three words, 58 suspect or flagged takes) and 15 clips without speech (silence, pink and white noise, and the last 0.35 s of ten takes). Times are a share of the audio's length, with 4 threads, and 2 for the embeddings. The laptop was rendering scratch chapters for phase 1 then, so its times run high.
**Decision:** The four models in `docs/machines.md`, "The take check's other models". AJ approved the list on 2026-11-12 and chose to put the files on both nodes at once, and they are there. The rest of this entry is Claude's choice.

* Silero VAD v5 (0.018 on the laptop, 0.010 on the Mac) never fired on noise, and it put the end of speech within about 0.2 s of Parakeet's last token on 95% of takes. TEN VAD called white noise at -50 and -35 dB speech. The smaller Silero file in the sherpa-onnx release ran speech on about 0.9 s past the last token in takes where the others agree it had ended.
* TitaNet small told the 14 voices apart best: 97.3% of takes lie nearest their own voice's centre, with an equal error rate of 0.113, at 0.009 on both nodes and 40 MB. TitaNet large (96.4%, 0.025) and ERes2Net from VoxCeleb (97.0%, 0.026) did as well at three times the time. ERes2NetV2 (97.0% but a margin of 0.17 against 0.29), WeSpeaker ResNet34 (90.2%) and ResNet221 (71.1%) and both CAM++ models (72.3% and 29.2%) did worse.
* Moonshine base English, from 2026-02-27, had a WER of 0.042 on the sample takes the check judged ok, where 3.0% were over 0.2, at 0.028 on the laptop and 0.020 on the Mac. It wrote text for 1 of the 15 clips without speech. The Zipformer from GigaSpeech was close on words (0.036) and faster, and wrote "THANK YOU" on silence. Whisper base.en wrote text for all 15. Qwen3-ASR 0.6B runs on the CPU in sherpa-onnx 2.0.0, so the plan's reason against it no longer holds, but it was five to eight times slower than Moonshine and wrote Chinese for three take tails. Parakeet's own WER on those takes was 0.023, low because its check chose them. On the 58 suspect or flagged takes, both recognizers heard 42 wrong. Moonshine heard several of the others right where Parakeet split or joined words, "Simon guessed" as "Simon Guest" and "Her mark" as "Hermark".
* ZIPA small CR-CTC 300k loads in sherpa-onnx's Zipformer CTC loader from ZIPA's own ONNX export, so neither node needs torch or lhotse. It gives IPA with token times at 0.014 on the laptop and 0.009 on the Mac. The models trained with noisy students are CC BY-NC-SA, so the CC BY one is the pick.
* The output depends on the node. Of 449 clips, the laptop and the Mac gave the same transcript for 416 with Parakeet and 413 with Moonshine, and the same phones for 312 of 434 with ZIPA. P4 has to compare a take with the pick on the node that heard it, or allow for this.
* A scratch pass over all 9,383 takes, on the laptop's CPU in 27 minutes, gave the starting data for packages P1 to P3: Silero hears speech more than 0.3 s past the last token in 110 takes. Over takes with 3 s of speech or more and no direction, the takes furthest from their voice's centre across the book are Anna Paimon's lines in chapters 7, 8 and 10 and Leonard Decarabia's chapter 7 line 133, which were rendered before those voices were recast.

### The take check flags a tail, a drifting voice and a take both recognizers hear wrong, and cuts a tail
**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Context:** Packages P1 to P3 of `docs/take-quality/phase-2.md`: sound after a take's last word, a take whose timbre drifts from its voice, and a second opinion on the suspect takes. Every number here comes from the 9,383 takes of chapters 7 to 48 of The Hundred Reigns in a scratch copy on 2026-11-12, heard with the four models of the entry before this one: chapters 7 to 26 on the laptop and 27 to 48 on the Mac. Under the rules of phase 1 those takes had 66 flagged and 22 suspect.
**Decision:** `takecheck.judge()` gains three reasons and a cut (`README.md`, Pipeline per chapter, step 5), and `JUDGE_V` is 4. Over the same takes the rules flag 81 and leave 20 suspect: `tail` 3, `drift` 10 and `garbled` 2 beside phase 1's reasons, and 21 takes in 16 chapters get a cut of 11.7 s in all. Version 3 of the rules, deployed first on 2026-11-12, also cut the tail of a line directed "purring with delight", and version 4 adds purring and 18 more sounds a direction can ask for, such as cackling, choking and hissing. The rest of this entry is Claude's choice, and AJ hasn't confirmed it.

* A tail is the sound after the line's last word that Parakeet's token times place, by the signal block's speech frames or the speech detector's segments, whichever ends later. 596 takes have sound more than 0.3 s past that word and 77 more than 0.5 s.
* Words heard after the line count only when the sound goes on more than 0.5 s past its last word. At 0.3 s the rule flagged 6 takes in chapters 7 to 26, and 4 of them were the second recognizer's way of hearing a last word: "keep, Anna" as "keep on, huh?", "right…" as "right there". At 0.5 s it flags 3 in the book, and each is the voice going on: "The stranger was a woman. Freeze.", "Lady Shabram flipped to another page. Jabbas", "Duchar replied with a sigh. Fossen.". A short line with an added word was already flagged as short, so the tail rule leaves lines of one to three words alone.
* The cut of a tail needs Parakeet, and the second recognizer when the take has its transcript, to hear the line's last word and no word after it, and a silent run between that word and the sound. It ends the take inside that run with a fade out, keeping half of PAUSE_KEEP. A line that asks for a sound after its words, by a vocal event or a direction such as laughing, coughing, sighing, weeping, purring or hissing, keeps its tail, and so does a line that keeps its pauses. The join's cut now takes a cut that reaches the end of the file. `JOIN_VERSION` stays 2, because AJ's backlog pass of 2026-11-12 was running, a new version would list every chapter for one more join, and the cuts touch 21 takes in 16 chapters. Instead a render records a hash of the cuts its join made, as `render.cuts`, and `breeze.render_changes` counts a join as due when the cuts a join would make now differ. A render from before that counts as having made every cut but one that ends a take, because only phase 2 makes such a cut. So the `backlog` list names the 16 chapters for their tails, and the app's skip of a repeated backlog render doesn't skip one whose tail is still to cut.
* The voice's centre is per chapter, over its takes with 3 s of speech or more of a line without a direction or a vocal event. A direction such as "echoing through the hall" or "deep, armored voice" moves the embedding on purpose, and the first run flagged those. A voice needs 8 such takes for a centre. A per chapter centre doesn't see a recast, because the chapter's takes all come from one clip, and the keys already send those lines to a render. The threshold is 10 median absolute deviations below the voice's median similarity, with the deviation at least 0.01, which flags 10 takes, 7 of them the narrator's, at 10.3 to 13.6 deviations. The centre counts every such take whatever its verdict, because the verdicts are judged against the baselines, and it leaves out the takes more than 4 deviations below a first centre, so a drifted take doesn't pull it.
* The second recognizer flags a suspect take of four words or more that it also hears wrong, by the word rules Parakeet's transcript goes through, names included. Of the 22 suspect takes, 2 are flagged that way: "I… I don't understand" heard by both as "I not understand", and "Duchar Honorius?" heard as "We saw Honorius" and "Do we sound Honorius". The other 20 are lines of one to three words. Moonshine hears 5 of those right ("Cry me a river", "Dark Saber", "Not thunder", "I will", "I see") and hears the same homophone as Parakeet in most of the rest: "Simon guessed" was heard as "Simon guest" nine times. So a short line stays suspect, as phase 1 decided for a changed word in one.
* Two takes rank by both recognizers' word errors when both have the second transcript, and by Parakeet's alone otherwise.
* Without the models a node gathers Parakeet's two blocks as before. The tail rule and the cut need only those, so they work there. `drift` and `garbled` need the speaker block and the second transcript. A take heard on a node with the models and one heard without them can get different verdicts, as a name's spelling already can between the two nodes.
* A model folder that lacks a file, or whose model doesn't load when the process first tries it, leaves its block out, and the check logs why. The take check still runs, and a saved take isn't heard again for a block the machine can't gather.
* `take_check.json` grows with the speaker block, from 302 KB to about 420 KB for a chapter of 190 takes.
* A scratch render on the Mac, with the models and the chapters' own copies of their takes, took lines 12, 13 (`tail`), 45 (`drift`) and 60 of chapter 44, lines 19, 187 and 199 (`drift`) of chapter 42, and lines 39 and 41 of chapter 33 again, and kept a new take for each. The new take of line 13 has no word after the line, and line 45's lies at 0.90 from its voice's centre against 0.75. Chapter 42's join cut the tails of lines 30 and 36 and came out at -18.1 LUFS with a true peak of -1.2 dB. A second render of the three would change nothing. Chapter 44 line 29, `garbled` in the gathering, was heard again on the Mac as "Duissar Honorius. Is that you?" and passed, so this verdict hangs on the node that hears the take, as a name's spelling does.

### The take check flags a name when both recognizers hear a sound its spelling doesn't have
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** Package P4 of `docs/take-quality/phase-2.md`: names that are said different ways. Parakeet's spellings alone can't show that, because it wrote Eole 87 ways. ZIPA small decodes phones with no text to guide it. AJ had picked "Eh-ole" and "Vwee-vruh" on 2026-11-12, each from one clip, and 31 chapters were rendered with them that night. The numbers here are from a scratch copy on the laptop on 2026-11-13: the library as containers had it that morning, and the 826 takes of the lines that say Eole, Vouivre, Louis or Lauriane.
**Decision:** The take check gathers a `phone` block for every take, and `takecheck.judge()` has the reason `name` (`README.md`, Pipeline per chapter, step 5): a take is flagged when Parakeet's spelling of a name and ZIPA's phones in its place both have a sound that the name's say-as spelling doesn't have. A `say-options` job screens each option in eight takes by the same rule. `JUDGE_V` is 7, and `takecheck.NAME_FLAGS` and `pronounce.SCREENS` are on. This is the second version of the rule. AJ marked a listening page for each version, and the second passed: of 25 fresh clips it flags, 23 sound wrong to him. The rest of this entry is Claude's choice.

**The first version, and why it went.** It measured how far ZIPA's phones around the name were from a reference saying, the usual saying of a screened option, with costs from the features of each phone. It was deployed at 09:40 and switched off at 10:13. AJ marked 40 clips without seeing the measure: 24 of Eole, 10 of Louis and 6 of Lauriane. His ear and the measure agreed on 23. Of the 17 it would take again, 10 sounded as picked to him, and of the 23 it passed, 7 sounded wrong, all of them Eole.

* The 10 false flags were takes where ZIPA's phones are not what he hears: `iɔl`, `etɪl` and `ɪtwəlz` for takes that say "Eh-ole" to him, and `louwɪz` for "Lewis". One recognizer's phones on one take are not enough to take a line again.
* Five of the 7 it passed and he rejected have a vowel after the name's last sound: "ay-oh-lee" and "ay-oh-lay". The reference was allowed to match any part of a window, so the measure couldn't tell an added syllable from the next word.
* His ear lets a vowel differ a good deal. `eɪl`, `iɔl` and `eoʊl` are all "Eh-ole" to him. It rejects an added syllable or consonant: "ay-oh-lee", "ay-hole", "yol", "so well". The first version weighed these the other way round.
* That distance still sorts the sayings on the Names tab, as a hint that judges nothing.

**The second version.**

* `phones.py` reads a skeleton from each source: the consonants in order, with one mark for each run of vowels. The reference is the skeleton of the spelling the voice is sent, read as English. "Eh-ole" is a vowel and an l. Parakeet's spelling of the saying gives one heard skeleton, and ZIPA's phones give the other.

### Judging a take again takes a fifth of the time, and the Names tab still reads the checks
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** Step N7 of `docs/take-quality/p4-name-check.md`. The Names tab counts only chapters whose check is current, because judging the library's saved takes again took over a minute, and the plan guessed that most of that was the token map. **Decision:** Claude's choice. Three changes make the judge faster and change no result, and the Names tab is left as it is.

* A profile put 60% of the time in `takecheck.align()`, 21% in `breeze.voice_text()` and 13% in `takecheck.word_times()`. The token map was not most of it.

### A job's time is estimated part by part, from each node's own timings
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** AJ asked for better time estimates for the running job and the queue. The queue showed a render's lines times the minutes a line took in the last three renders, whatever those renders made and whichever node ran them. Over sixteen touch-up renders of that evening's batch, thirteen on aj-legion5 and three on jsc-m1, the figure was off by 45% at the median and 228% at worst, and it read 553 s for a render that took 194 s. A running render showed 60 s left for the whole of its join, and 97% from the end of its takes on. The scheduler's own figure, takes times the node's rate plus 90 s and half a second a line, was off by about 20% at the median and 92% at worst on the day's earlier renders. Timed on aj-legion5 over those thirteen: 6 s from the dispatch to the step, 21 to 36 s from there to the end of the first take, 2.8 to 4.9 s for each take after it, a check before the join of about 20 s and 1.8 s a take that grows with each flagged take, 51 to 60 s to join a chapter of 3,300 to 4,300 words, and 2 to 8 s to send the audio back. One join took 129 s while other work used the laptop's CPU. The first render timed on jsc-m1 took 96 s to the end of its first take, 73 s of it that take, and 16 s for each take after it. The second made its takes at 39 s each. The script's line count predicted none of it.
**Decision:** The app notes when the first log line of each part of a job arrives (`jobs.LOG_MARKS`). A stage that succeeds adds each part's seconds and size to `timing` in `placement.json` for its node, the last 12 of a kind. `nomad.expected()` makes the estimate: the default until a node has three timings, and from then on the default scaled by the median ratio of the node's timings to it, which one job that ran long doesn't move. A Breeze render is costed as its setup, which runs to the end of the first take, the takes after it, the check before the join, the join by the chapter's words and the sending back. A render that makes under half of its chapter's lines is a touch-up, and its takes and its check are costed from the node's touch-ups, because the lines a touch-up makes run longer and are flagged more often. A whole chapter keeps the take rate of the entry above. The scheduler, a running job's time left and the queue's forecast read that one estimate. The time left runs to the end of the job's last stage and counts down between log lines, and the bar is the share of the job's expected time that has passed. The forecast places each queued job by the scheduler's rules in outline and gives its start, its end and its node. It doesn't count takes. The app counts two queued renders a tick outside its lock, at about half a second a chapter on containers: the takes each would make or take again, and the saved takes it would only hear, which after a new version of the check is every take of the chapter. A render shows no time until it is counted. A forecast over a long queue runs less often, so that it holds the lock for a twentieth of the time at most: a pass over 300 queued renders took 0.13 s on the laptop. Replayed over the same sixteen renders, the estimate before a job starts was off by 11% at the median and 47% at worst, the render on jsc-m1 whose takes ran at 39 s. The time left was off by 26 s at the median, against 62 s for the figure the page showed. Most of what is left is the takes a check flags, which nobody knows before the check. Once the check says how many it flagged, the time left is that round of retakes. Rejected: a fit of a job's whole time, which needs no marks and can't say how long the current part has left. Also rejected: a line of each node's own through six or more timings, which on nine renders was off by more than the scaled default on the check, 35 s against 28 s at the mean, and no closer on the takes.

### The text a voice is sent gets no new repair

* Parentheses read well. `speakable` turns them into commas before the voice sees them, and the 41 lines with takes that have them, such as "(passive)" in a perk's name, have 0.09 errors per 100 words and no flag.

### A heteronym gets no respelling and no check
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** Package P5 of `docs/take-quality/phase-2.md` asked how often Breeze misreads a heteronym, a word such as "read" or "lead" whose reading the context decides, before anything was built for it. The package would have respelled the rarer sense of a marked line in `breeze.voice_text()`, which needs the marks pass for every script, one Claude pass a chapter, and it would have checked the take between the two readings with ZIPA's phones. The measurement is over the saved takes of The Hundred Reigns as the library had them on 2026-11-13 at 13:10: 8,888 takes with 146,149 words, of which 1,149 are one of 118 spellings that have a second reading.
**Decision:** P5 stops at its measurement, as its plan says to when a misread is rare. `voice_text()` stays as it is, so no key changes. The marks pass still has no job, and no rule reads a heteronym. This is Claude's choice, and AJ hasn't confirmed it.

* Breeze reads a heteronym by its sense. The audio could judge 253 sayings, and 249 of them have the reading their line means. That is "read" 28 times of 28, 16 in the past tense and 12 in the present, "lead" 20 of 20, "live", "lived" and "lives" 43 of 43, "wind" and "winds" 14 of 14, "tear" and "tears" 17 of 18, "bow", "bowed" and "bows" 15 of 15 with 11 bends and 4 weapons, "wound" and "wounds" 14 of 15, "minute", "row" and "sow" 11 of 11, and 87 of 89 for the pairs that differ in stress: "contract", "content", "conduct", "conflict", "object", "present", "produce", "progress", "project" and "record".
* The other 4 are doubtful and not known misreads: "tears" in chapter 21 line 205, "wounds" in chapter 37 line 252, and "object" in chapter 12 line 123 and chapter 16 line 71. Each lies near the limit of the audio check, which is itself wrong on 1 to 5% of plain words. At worst that is 4 misreads in 40 chapters.
* The audio check is a linear discriminant on the cepstrum of the word's vowel, one for each contrast, trained on plain words of the same takes that have the contrast: "need" against "said", "give" against "five", "constant" against "control". Scored on each control word with that word held out, it is right for 93 to 100% of them on 18 of the 20 sides, and for 89% and 82% on the other two. What shows it hears the reading is that it agrees with the grammar on words that have both senses in the book, as with the two tenses of "read". AJ's ear agrees. He marked a page of 26 clips on 2026-11-13, with the 4 doubtful sayings, 8 that ZIPA writes with the other reading, and 14 from the two groups below, and he heard the word said right in all 26.
* ZIPA can't judge a heteronym. It writes one reading of a spelling whatever the take says: "live" as "lyve" in 21 takes of 22, "wind" as "wynd" in 20 of 21, "lead" as "led" in 20 of 22, "lived" as "lyved", and "reading" as "redding" in 12 of 13. It agrees with the audio check on 131 of the 253 sayings, and it has the tense of "read" right in 12 of 28. On plain words it is right: 99% of 9,074 control words in six contrasts. The cause is probably that its training transcripts came from a pronouncing dictionary, which has one reading for a spelling. So the check that P5 planned between two readings would need another measure, and any rule that reads ZIPA's phones of a real word gets the dictionary's reading of it. A name with an invented spelling doesn't have one.
* Two groups are not measured by the audio check, and AJ heard all 14 clips of them on his page said right: 8 of "use" and "close", and 6 of "learned" and "cursed". For the pairs that differ in a final s or z ("use" 77 times, "house" 76, "close" 37, "excuse" 9), the audio check is right for only 86 to 89% of held-out words. "close" follows its sense in 21 of 24 lines and "use" in 50 of 64, which is about what that check gets right. For the words whose -ed can be a syllable ("cursed" 35 times, "learned" 32, "winged", "blessed" and "aged"), it is right for 83% of the one-syllable control words. ZIPA writes "learned" with two syllables in 23 takes of 32, and the audio check hears one syllable in 18 of 22. The one metal "lead" of the book, in chapter 51, is not in the scratch copy that has audio.

**Date:** 2026-11-12

### A chapter job waits for a character pass only on its own chapter
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** AJ queued the Process batch for chapters 54 to 83 of The Hundred Reigns at 20:52. Its 30 character passes ran one after the other on dev-arch until 21:28. For those 36 minutes no screenplay was written and no GPU node had a job, though chapter 53 was scripted and its render was in the batch. AJ asked "Shouldn't screenplay and render for a chapter only depend on the character pass for that chapter, not the character pass for all chapters?". A screenplay is sent its chapter's `characters_present`, which its own chapter's pass writes, with the names and aliases of those characters. A render reads the voices of its script's speakers and the say-as spellings. A pass of another chapter adds characters, facts, aliases and pronunciation terms. It takes nothing away and writes no file of the job's chapter. Two things a screenplay is sent can still differ with the moment it is written: a later pass sets the serial's narration when its chapter is the first to name a first-person narrator, and a chapter with no characters present is sent the whole bible as it stands. The rule of 2026-11-11 gave as its reason that a chapter job reads the bible when its bundle is built. What that guards against is a result that lands while a bundle is built, and the first point below covers it without a wait. That AJ wants the wait gone is Claude's reading of his question.
**Decision:** On the cluster a screenplay, a render or a take check waits for a character pass only when the pass works on its chapter, whether the pass runs or is queued ahead of it. Character passes still wait for each other and for a cast.

* `exchange.build_bundle` gives each shipped file the hash of the bytes it puts in the zip. It took the hashes first and read the files afterwards, so a result that landed in between left a file in the bundle that didn't match its hash, and the worker then reported that file as its own change. A character pass can now end while a screenplay's bundle is built. Since 2026-11-11 the same could happen to a character pass that fetched its bundle while a render's result landed. A file that is gone by the time it is read, such as the temporary file of a result that is landing, is left out of the bundle. It made the fetch fail before, and the worker asked again.
* A screenplay doesn't wait for a cast either, nor for casting bios, a voice pick or a voice effect (`NO_SCRIPT_INPUT` in `serial_audio/web/nomad.py`). This is Claude's choice, and AJ hasn't confirmed it. A batch queues its one cast ahead of its screenplays, and the cast waits for every character pass, so with that wait the screenplays would still have started after the last pass. The one thing a screenplay read that a cast writes was the `voiced` column of its cast list, and the prompt has no rule for the column. The column is gone, so a script is the same whether it is written before the cast or after it. Of the 11,704 script lines in the library on 2026-11-13, 125 have a speaker without a voice, so the pass named such speakers with the column too. A blind review's probe found the pass's input the same after a change of `voice_status`, `bio`, `narrator_voice_id` and `voice_effects`. A screenplay still waits for a say-as, which changes the pronunciations it is sent.
* A render and a take check still wait for a cast, which gives the chapter's speakers their voices. In a batch the cast runs after the last character pass, so the batch's renders still start after all of its character passes and the cast. The next entry changes that for the renders.
* In a batch a chapter's screenplay starts when its own character pass has ended, beside the later passes and the cast. A character pass that can start takes a free Claude place before a screenplay does, because it is earlier in the queue.
* A failed character pass or import no longer skips the screenplays of the chapters before it. Their renders are skipped with the cast.
* A job whose record names no chapter counts as working on every chapter, so it and a character pass wait for each other. A record names none when the chapter was not on disk as the job was queued, and no import of it was queued ahead in its batch. The blind review found that a screenplay queued so started beside its own chapter's pass.
* A job that waits for a running character pass of its own chapter, or a pass that waits for such a job, reads "which works on the same chapter" in the queue. It read "which changes the whole serial".
* The one-machine executor is unchanged.
* The change was deployed on 2026-11-13 at 22:03, as the copy containers ran plus the diff of three files, because the tree held another session's unfinished work in `web/nomad.py`. The screenplays of chapters 81 and 82 fetched their bundles after the deploy, and each wrote back its own two files with nothing reported unchanged or in conflict. The batch's character passes and its cast had ended by then, so on the cluster no screenplay has started beside a character pass yet. The unit tests cover that, and the fake Nomad test ran on containers, because a cast was using the laptop's GPU. An Opus blind review found nothing above low. The Codex review didn't run, because Codex had hit its usage limit.

### A batch casts after each chapter's character pass
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** After the entry above, every render of a batch still waited for the batch's one cast, and the cast waited for every character pass. In the batch of chapters 54 to 83 of The Hundred Reigns the cast could start 36 minutes after the batch was queued. A rule that lets a render pass the cast when none of its chapter's characters waits for a voice would have freed 3 of those 30 chapters, counted by the characters present in each, because hector-honorius alone is present in 12 of them. So a render starts early only when its voices are cast early. Claude asked AJ "cast chapter by chapter?", with the cost that the next pass waits for each cast and that each cast needs a free GPU node, and AJ answered "Yes". About 11 of the 30 chapters brought a new character to cast.
**Decision:** The Process dialog, and an import that processes its chapters, queue a cast after each character pass, ahead of the batch's screenplays and renders.

* The first cast of a batch is for everyone who needs a voice, and every render waits for it, as before. It also takes the characters of older chapters who still need a voice. Each later cast is for everyone who needs a voice by then too. It names the chapter whose pass it follows, as `from_chapter` in its params and its job record, and reads "Cast · ch 55" in the queue. A render or a take check of earlier chapters only doesn't wait for such a cast, whether it is queued or running (`VOICE_READERS` in `conflicts()`). So chapter 54's render waits for the passes and casts up to chapter 54 and for its screenplay.
* A character pass and a cast still wait for each other, because both write `bible/characters.yaml`. The passes and casts of a batch therefore run one after the other in chapter order, and the cast that follows a chapter's pass finds the bible as that pass left it. The cast command is unchanged.
* A cast for everyone that finds nobody who needs a voice is skipped when its turn comes, with the note "nobody needs a voice, so there is nothing to cast". Before, it was dispatched to the Claude node and then to a GPU node, where the command said "nothing to cast". This holds for a cast queued by hand and on the one-machine executor too. The app reads the serial and its bible for it once per cast, which took 0.75 s for The Hundred Reigns on 2026-11-13.
* The passes and casts are queued first, so they take a free Claude place and a free GPU node before the batch's screenplays and renders. A cast's voices stage still waits for a GPU node while every node renders, and the next pass waits with it.
* What AJ was told it costs: a chain of passes that takes longer, because each pass waits for the cast before it. What he was not told before he answered: a character who becomes castable only by a later chapter's pass, promoted to recurring or speaking for the first time, has the narrator read their lines in the chapters of the batch that rendered before that cast. With one cast at the end those chapters had the voice. A later render of such a chapter makes only those lines again.
* A failed cast skips the casts after it and the renders from its chapter on. The passes, the screenplays and the renders of earlier chapters go on, because a character pass waits for a cast only to write the bible after it and reads nothing the cast files. A blind review found that the first version skipped the later passes and their screenplays too.
* The estimates count a cast that follows a chapter's pass as one character, where a cast for everyone counts as three (`_cast_units` in `serial_audio/web/nomad.py`). The forecast still counts a cast that will be skipped as such a cast, because nobody knows before the pass whether it brings someone to cast.
* Two things the blind review found are left as they are. A cast with nobody to cast is skipped only when a Claude place is free for it, so it can wait behind three Claude jobs of another serial. And a render of an earlier chapter can fetch its bundle in the instant a later cast's result is written, between the bundle's listing of the files and its first read. If a character of that cast speaks in the render's chapter, the render fails because the voice has no record, and it has to be queued again.
* The change was deployed on 2026-11-13 at 22:40, as the copy containers ran plus the diff of `web/nomad.py`, `web/jobs.py` and `static/app.js`. The unit tests and the fake Nomad test, run on containers, cover the order of a batch, the skipped cast and a failed cast. No batch has run on the cluster with it yet. An Opus blind review found nothing high, and its two medium findings are the failed cast and the estimate above. The Codex review didn't run, because Codex had hit its usage limit that evening.

### A worker runs its node's Breeze jobs back to back, and what it loaded stays up
**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Context:** AJ asked whether jobs of one kind could reuse state, because each job set itself up, did its work and shut down alone. Every Breeze job started breeze-server and loaded the model, and every pass of the take check started a Parakeet process that loaded up to five models. Timed on aj-legion5 with nothing else running: the check that Parakeet can run took 1.9 s, and a render made it once before its takes and once before every pass. Loading the four optional models to see that they load took 3.3 s a render. Each pass spent 7.8 s before it heard its first take, at 2.3 passes a render. That is about 27 s a render. breeze-server took 6 s to start. Before it, a render made three requests to a ComfyUI that was down and the worker made a fourth, and each took 2 s, because Windows refuses a connection to a closed port of the same machine only after 2 s. A touch-up render of that evening took 272 s at the median, so about a sixth of it was start-up. On jsc-m1 breeze-server takes 21 to 40 s to start, and on dev-arch 20 s.
**Decision:** Three changes, and each works without the others. A request to ComfyUI or breeze-server on the same machine first asks whether the port accepts a connection, and gives up after half a second (`machine.listening()`). A process that hears with Parakeet starts one `parakeet_asr.py --serve` and sends it every job over a port of 127.0.0.1, with a token, so each model loads once in a render, a cast and the rounds of a name's options (`asr.serving()`). When that process can't start or ends early, each call runs a process of its own as before. And a worker that finishes a `breeze` job sends the version of its code with the result. The app then runs a scheduling pass and hands the worker the first job that the pass would dispatch to that node as a `breeze` job, when the worker runs the app's code. The worker fetches that job's bundle and runs it in the same Nomad task. It keeps the GPU lock from its first job to its last, and the breeze-server and the Parakeet process that its steps started stay up between the jobs, in a registry beside the lock (`kept.py`). The worker stops them when the app doesn't hand it a job, when a job fails or is stopped, when a handed job has other code than its own, and before it waits for something outside the job. A step that finds ComfyUI busy stops the kept breeze-server before it waits. On Windows the kept processes also join a job object that the worker holds, so they end with the worker however it ends. Elsewhere the next worker that takes the lock stops what the registry still names. A handed job counts as dispatched under the worker's Nomad job, so the app follows it, stops it and loses it by that task, and a handed job whose task ends before it fetched the bundle is queued again. `HAND_NEXT` in `serial_audio/web/nomad.py` and `SERVE` in `serial_audio/asr.py` switch the two off. The Nomad job and `bootstrap.py` didn't change, so no job was registered again. Measured on aj-legion5 with the real server and recognizer, a step that makes two takes and hears them twice took 20.8 s as the first job of a task and 6.1 s as each job after it. The code before would take about 43 s for it every time. The first handovers on the cluster showed a fourth cost, in the app. The scheduling pass after each result counted the takes of every queued render inside the app's lock, because the scheduler dropped its own counts whenever a job finished. With 28 renders of new chapters queued that took 22 s, during which the app answered nobody, and it sat between each result and the next job on that node, handed or dispatched. `_takes_needed()` now reads the counts that `_poll_work()` keeps outside the lock, and counts a render itself only when nobody has. With all of it live, the first take of chapter 58 of The Hundred Reigns was done 14.6 s after chapter 57 ended on aj-legion5: 3 s for the app to build the bundle, 5.5 s to fetch and unpack its 36 MB, 3 s to plan the chapter and 3 s for the take. Between chapters 53 and 54, before the change, that took 45.6 s. A whole chapter takes about 1,300 s there, so the handover saves it about 2%, and the check before the join of 250 takes took as long as before. A touch-up render of 272 s should save about 17 s at the handover, because the backlog renders of that evening didn't stall the app, and about 20 s of recognizer starts, which is about a seventh. That is worked out from the parts timed above, and the six touch-up renders that ran that night are too few to measure it. Of the 36 renders that ended after the first deploy, at 22:12, none failed and 26 ran handed, each with the breeze-server that was up. From the end of the job before to its first finished take, without that take itself, a handed render took 12.6 s at the median on aj-legion5, 8.3 s on jsc-m1 and 6.9 s on dev-arch. A dispatched render took 24.9 s, 30.3 s and 31.7 s from its dispatch. `docs/pipelining.md` has what is left to check and to decide. What the handing costs: while a worker has had a job handed to it, breeze-server holds 3.8 GB of the laptop's card through that job's join and the next job's start, where the card was free for about a minute between two renders before.
Rejected: a breeze-server that outlives its task and stops after some idle time. It holds the card with no job to answer for it, it needs a rule for letting go when ComfyUI wants the card, and on dev-arch Nomad ends every process of a task with the task. Also rejected: a worker that waits for work after its result. It holds the card and a Nomad allocation while it waits, and the app would have to know that the node is taken.
