# Automated prosody checks for single TTS takes: emphasis, intonation, pacing and delivery against a direction (state as of November 2026)

Scope: detecting prosody defects in one synthetic English utterance, knowing what the prosody should have been, and fixing it when the TTS (Breeze TTS 2 via breeze-server) takes only text, a direction and a seed. Dropped or repeated words, phoneme-level pronunciation, loudness and artifacts are out of scope (other researchers). All numbers are as published by the authors unless marked otherwise. "Inference" bullets are my reasoning, not published results.

## 1. Which tools detect the emphasized or prominent word, and how do they behave on synthetic speech?

### Takeaway
WhiStress (Interspeech 2025, a frozen whisper-small.en plus a 7M-parameter stress head, CC-BY-NC-4.0) is the most usable open detector: F1 0.96 on read speech, 0.69 zero-shot on expressive real speech, and when CAST (April 2026) validated it on TTS output it agreed with human listeners at kappa 0.32, while the humans agreed with each other at only Fleiss kappa 0.36. That is good enough to rank several takes of one line, not good enough to fail a single take on its own. Audio-language models are far worse at locating the stressed word (Gemini 2.5 Pro F1 about 40 to 50), and the fine-tuned StresSLM (7B) beats them on its own data but trails WhiStress on real expressive speech.

### Cited findings
- WhiStress extends Whisper with a stress detection head that labels each transcription token as stressed or not, alignment-free, on the whisper-small.en backbone, by Yosha, Shteyman and Adi, Interspeech 2025, arXiv 2505.19103 (May 2025) - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1), [project page](https://pages.cs.huji.ac.il/adiyoss-lab/whistress)
- Architecture: frozen Whisper backbone, an extra Whisper decoder block with cross-attention plus a two-layer classifier, about 7M trainable parameters. Input from encoder layer 9 worked best (F1 by layer: 3 = 0.693, 6 = 0.845, 9 = 0.909, 12 = 0.884) - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)
- Training data TinyStress-15K (about 15 h) is synthetic: TinyStories sentences, GPT-4o-mini proposed the stressed words, Google TTS with SSML rendered them with slower rate, louder volume and +1.5 semitone pitch on the stressed word, 10 voices. The authors say the raw TTS sounded robotic and they tuned settings by hand - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)
- WhiStress word-level results (P / R / F1): TinyStress-15K 0.912 / 0.906 / 0.909. Aix-MARSEC 0.953 / 0.968 / 0.961 (the paper notes this set is easy because function words are almost always unstressed). Expresso zero-shot 0.573 / 0.863 / 0.689 versus EmphaClass 0.569 / 0.769 / 0.654. EmphAssess zero-shot 0.672 / 0.98 / 0.797, in-domain 0.945 / 0.942 / 0.943 versus EmphaClass 0.938 - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)
- Zero-shot WhiStress has high recall and lower precision on expressive speech (Expresso P 0.573, R 0.863), so it tends to mark extra words as stressed - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)
- Weights: Hugging Face slprl/WhiStress, licence cc-by-nc-4.0, "research use only", files additional_decoder_block.pt and classifier.pt, run through WhiStressInferenceClient(device="cuda" or "cpu"). Passing transcription=None decodes text and stress, passing the known transcript predicts stress only. Collection dated 25 to 27 May 2025. No hosted inference - [WhiStress model card](https://huggingface.co/slprl/WhiStress)
- No inference speed is reported in the WhiStress paper - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)
- CAST validated WhiStress on its TTS benchmark audio: annotator Fleiss kappa 0.36, human versus WhiStress kappa 0.32 (F1 0.48), inside the pairwise annotator range 0.29 to 0.40 - [CAST, arXiv 2604.10580](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)
- EmphAssess (de Seyssel, D'Avirro, Williams, Dupoux, Meta AI and ENS, EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2312.14069 v1 Dec 2023) ships EmphaClass, an emphasis classifier at frame or word level fine-tuned on English from a multilingual self-supervised speech model, released open source with the evaluation code and data - [EmphAssess paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14069v2), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.30)
- StressTest (Yosha, Maimon, Adi, arXiv 2505.22765, revised Oct 2025, listed for ACL 2026): 101 texts, 218 samples by one professional actor, plus StressPresso (202 samples from Expresso). Tasks: Sentence Stress Detection (SSD, which words are stressed) and Sentence Stress Reasoning (SSR, which meaning is intended) - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- SSD F1 (%) on Expresso / StressPresso / StressTest: WhiStress 68.9 / 83.5 / 88.5, StresSLM 59.1 / 80.6 / 87.0, Gemini-2.5-Pro 39.6 / 40.7 / 50.5, gpt-4o-audio 34.7 / 36.9 / 40.5, Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct 32.3 / 25.5 / 32.1, LLaMA-Omni 28.3 / 23.8 / 32.0, Phi-4-multimodal 28.2 / 26.0 / 24.7, SALMONN 20.5 / 23.0 / 23.2 - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- Version conflict: a search summary of the paper gives Gemini-2.5-Pro SSD 48.5 and StresSLM SSD 86.9, while the v2 HTML gives 50.5 and 87.0. Probably v1 versus v2 numbers - [StressTest search summary, arXiv 2505.22765](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22765), contradicted by [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- StresSLM is Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16) on Stress-17k plus ASR and emotion rehearsal data, with no reported ASR or emotion loss. Human SSD performance is not reported. The page states no licence for the released weights - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- Behaviour of a commercial TTS on stress markup: Stress-17k was synthesized with OpenAI's TTS API using asterisks around the stressed word, and the authors report frequent stress errors in the raw output. A human check found the WhiStress-verified subset matched the intended stress much better (SSD F1 78.8% versus 50.8% unverified). Only about 1.3K of about 4.4K generated samples passed verification (the text elsewhere says about 4K of 4.5K, an internal inconsistency) - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- WhiStress is already used as an automatic verifier and reward: StressTest filters synthetic data with it, CAST scores TTS systems with it, and EmphTTS (Oct 2026) uses WhiStress balanced accuracy as an RL reward and StressLM (the Qwen2-Audio-based model) as the evaluation F1 - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2), [CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580), [EmphTTS, arXiv 2609.27599](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27599)
- Wavelet Prosody Toolkit (WPT) builds a composite signal from pitch, energy and duration with a continuous wavelet transform and reads continuous word prominence off lines of maximum amplitude. It is used as an automatic labeller, for example for word prominence targets on ARCTIC and L2-ARCTIC in a wav2vec2 probing study - [accent-ID probing, arXiv 2306.06524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06524)
- The Helsinki Prosody Corpus labels (LibriTTS, 2.8M words) were generated automatically, not by listeners - [Talman et al., arXiv 1908.02262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.02262)
- A wav2vec2 XLS-R word prominence detector trained with CTC (Linke and Schuppler, Sept 2025) reached 85.53% accuracy on Austrian German (GRASS) on utterances whose words were recognized correctly - [arXiv 2509.10116](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10116v1)

### Inferences
- Because WhiStress agrees with a listener about as well as two listeners agree with each other on TTS audio, a single take's verdict is noisy. The useful form is relative: among N takes of the same line, prefer takes where the intended focus word is marked stressed and the words that would change the meaning are not. Use it as a ranking score, not as a pass or fail gate.
- Pass the script text as the transcription (the client supports it), so the stress head labels the intended tokens and the existing Parakeet check owns word accuracy.
- WhiStress learned stress from exaggerated SSML cues (slower, louder, +1.5 semitone). Breeze's subtler natural stress may be under-detected, and a loud or slow word may be over-detected. Its zero-shot precision of 0.57 on expressive speech means many false "stressed" marks, so only check the target word and a short list of competitor words rather than demanding an exact stress set.
- whisper-small sized models usually run on CPU in about real time or faster, so a few seconds per line is plausible, but this is unmeasured. It should be timed on the laptop CPU and the M1 before committing.
- The CC-BY-NC-4.0 licence fits a personal hobby pipeline but rules out any commercial use.

### Gaps
- No published CPU latency, ONNX export or sherpa-onnx port of WhiStress, StresSLM or EmphaClass was found.
- No study of any stress detector on Breeze TTS 2 output or on Qwen3-TTS-codec speech specifically.
- EmphaClass's backbone, size, licence and repository were not verified.
- StresSLM weight licence not stated on the pages read.
- No published word-level accuracy for the Wavelet Prosody Toolkit against listeners was confirmed in this search.

## 2. How can the pipeline know which word should be emphasized?

### Takeaway
A text LLM predicts the context-required stressed word well when it sees the preceding context: Claude Haiku 4.5 hit the intended word 88.1% of the time on CAST, against 25 to 58% for small Qwen3 models. The ground truth is soft, though. CAST's own annotators agreed with each other 79% of the time, and the EME-TTS authors state that multiple valid emphasis positions may exist. The Helsinki prominence line predicts general prominence, not contrastive focus, at about 83% accuracy against automatic labels. Author emphasis in the source (EPUB italics) is the highest-precision signal, but nothing measures it.

### Cited findings
- Helsinki Prosody Corpus (University of Helsinki, 2019): automatically generated prominence labels for about 260 h and 2.8M words of LibriTTS audiobooks from 1230 speakers. BERT outperforms the other models even with less than 10% of the training data. Code and data at Helsinki-NLP/prosody - [Talman et al., arXiv 1908.02262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.02262)
- Reported accuracies on that benchmark: BERT 83.2, BiLSTM 82.1, CRF (MarMoT) 81.8, SVM (Minitagger) 80.8. These come from a leaderboard mirror - [hyper.ai leaderboard mirror](https://hyper.ai/ja/sota/tasks/prosody-prediction/benchmark/prosody-prediction-on-helsinki-prosody-corpus)
- Stephenson et al. (Interspeech 2022) fine-tuned BERT to predict prominence on personal pronouns (contrastive focus) and fed the labels to a TTS prominence control, and tested whether earlier utterances help - [arXiv 2207.01718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.01718), [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/stephenson22_interspeech.html)
- CAST, "Knowing What to Stress: A Discourse-Conditioned Text-to-Speech Benchmark" (Turetzky, Dekel, Aronowitz, Hoory, Adi, Hebrew University and IBM Research, arXiv 2604.10580, 12 Apr 2026, CC BY 4.0): 113 contrastive pairs (226 items) where the same sentence needs a different stressed word after different contexts. Each sentence has exactly two plausible stress candidates. Items were generated with gpt-5-mini and kept only when gpt-5-nano and gemini-2.5-flash agreed on the stressed word. A 10k synthetic training corpus is also described. Code release is promised, no URL given - [CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)
- CAST text-only results (Hit / Pair-Contrast / Pair-Correct, %): Qwen3-0.6B 25.2 / 25.2 / 5.3, Qwen3-1.7B 50.0 / 50.0 / 24.8, Qwen3-4B 57.5 / 57.5 / 37.2, Claude-haiku-4.5 88.1 / 88.1 / 76.1 - [CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)
- CAST human labels: three annotators scored 80 to 94% individually, 79% average pairwise agreement. A majority (2 of 3) existed on 94% of items and matched the ground truth on 93.6% of those. The three items where the majority diverged had multiple plausible focal words. The paper does not quantify how often several placements are acceptable - [CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)
- EME-TTS (Li, Qu, Hu, Li, arXiv 2507.12015, July 2025): at inference an LLM (the paper cites GPT-4) predicts emphasis positions from the emotion label and the text. No accuracy against human labels is reported. The authors state emphasis identification is subjective and "multiple valid emphasis positions may exist" - [EME-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12015v1)
- EME-TTS also found emphasis interacts with emotion: emphasis made neutral speech sound emotional, and surprise was hard to recognize because its final pitch rise made listeners hear emphasis at the sentence end - [EME-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12015v1)
- Once the stress is known, a text LLM interprets it well: StressTest SSR with transcript and gold stress given as text, gpt-4o 86.2% (StressTest) and 83.6% (StressPresso). Human listeners 92.6% overall and 96.0% by majority vote - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)

### Inferences
- The existing Claude screenplay pass already sees the chapter context. It could emit, per line, an optional focus field: the intended focus word, any acceptable alternatives, and the words whose stress would change the meaning. CAST suggests a frontier Claude model gets this right in roughly 9 of 10 contrastive cases, and more cheaply than any audio check.
- Most narration has broad focus, where the default accent on the last content word is fine and many placements pass. Restricting the stress check to lines Claude marks as narrow or contrastive focus (corrections, "not X but Y", contrasted pronouns, echo questions, author italics) avoids acting on soft ground truth.
- EPUB and web-serial HTML italics (`<em>`, `<i>`) inside dialogue are explicit author emphasis. Carrying them into the script as a focus mark is a near-free, high-precision target, separate from italics used for thoughts, titles or foreign words, which Claude would have to tell apart.

### Gaps
- No measurement of how often more than one stress placement is acceptable in ordinary fiction dialogue or narration.
- No data on how often web serials use italics for emphasis versus thoughts or titles.
- No 2025 or 2026 study comparing LLM-predicted and human emphasis on audiobook text specifically. CAST is short constructed sentences with two candidates each.

## 3. How good are audio-language models as judges of prosody, emotion and naturalness?

### Takeaway
Not good enough to act on per take without a human. They rank whole TTS systems well (EmergentTTS-Eval: Spearman 0.905 against human rankings for Gemini and GPT-4o-audio judges), but per-sample verdicts are near the noise floor: on word stress the best (Gemini 2.5 Pro) gets SSD F1 40 to 50, on pairwise naturalness the best ALM agrees with the human label 69.1% of the time, and on VoxParadox, where the voice contradicts the words, every model scores under 31% because they read the transcript instead of listening. Claude does not accept audio input as of November 2026. The ALMs that run locally in llama.cpp and fit 8 GB (Qwen2.5-Omni 3B or 7B, Voxtral Mini 3B, Gemma 4 E2B or E4B, Ultravox) are among the weakest judges measured.

### Cited findings
Benchmarks and agreement
- EmergentTTS-Eval (Boson AI, arXiv 2505.23009, May 2025, NeurIPS 2025): 1,645 cases in six categories (Questions, Emotions, Paralinguistics, Foreign Words, Syntactic Complexity, Complex Pronunciation). An ALM judge compares a candidate with gpt-4o-mini-tts (Alloy) on the same text, with random T1/T2 labels against position bias, returns justifications, a 0 to 3 score per system and a winner, at temperature 0. Gemini 2.5 Pro is the main judge - [EmergentTTS-Eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23009v1)
- EmergentTTS-Eval human agreement is measured on system rankings (512 pairs, 8 raters): Spearman 0.905 for Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash and GPT-4o-audio, 0.881 Qwen 2.5 Omni, 0.762 GPT-4o-mini-audio. Gemini 2.5 Pro overestimated Hume AI and Sesame1B. Qwen 2.5 Omni as judge had frequent parsing failures and win rates near 50%. Judges sometimes hallucinate pronunciation errors on subjective dimensions. Cost about $50 per full system evaluation with Gemini 2.5 Pro. The judge rates prosody, intonation, stress and pausing, but there is no word-level stress metric - [EmergentTTS-Eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23009v1)
- SpeechJudge (AmphionTeam, arXiv 2511.07931, ICLR 2026): pairwise naturalness on 99K pairs. Human inter-annotator agreement is only 50.7% on ternary labels (A, B, tie) and 69.0% on binary preferences with ties removed (English 61.7%) - [SpeechJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07931v2)
- SpeechJudge-Eval accuracy (%): Gemini-2.5-Flash 69.1, GPT-4o Audio 67.4, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct 67.0, Gemini-2.5-Pro 66.5, MiDashengLM 61.6, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 60.6, Phi-4-Multimodal 57.0, Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507 56.0, MiMo-Audio-7B 54.1, GPT-4o mini Audio 50.5, Gemma-3n-E4B-it 48.2. Objective metrics: WER 57.9, DNSMOS 57.9, UTMOS 53.7, Audiobox CE 60.8. SpeechJudge-GRM (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B Thinker, SFT then RL with LoRA) 77.2%, 79.4% with 10-vote majority, Bradley-Terry baseline 72.7% - [SpeechJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07931v2)
- StressTest SSR accuracy (%), StressTest / StressPresso: Gemini-2.5-Pro 77.9 / 72.7, gpt-4o-audio 58.7 / 64.8, Qwen2-Audio-7B 56.4 / 51.4, SALMONN 56.8 / 52.4, LLaMA-Omni 53.6 / 52.9, Phi-4-multimodal 53.2 / 50.4, StresSLM 86.2 / 87.6, humans 92.6 (96.0 majority). SSD F1 is in section 1 - [StressTest paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)
- VoxParadox, "Do Audio LLMs Listen or Read?" (Pang, Chaubey, Soleymani, arXiv 2605.27772, May 2026, ICML 2026): 2,000 synthetic TTS examples across 10 paralinguistic tasks where the voice contradicts the transcript. Models show consistently low accuracy on acoustic ground truth and pick the answer the words imply. Probing finds paralinguistic cues fade in deeper encoder layers and at the encoder-to-LLM interface, and the LLM often ignores cues that survive. A layer mixer plus DPO raised Audio Flamingo 3 from 17.40% to 65.20% - [VoxParadox abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27772)
- VoxParadox per-task scores (%, self-reported, aggregator table, average / emotion / speed / intonation): Gemini 2.5 Flash 24.7 / 6 / 16 / 19, GPT-4o Audio 8.6 / 0 / 8 / 0.5, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 7.95 / 3 / 8 / 4.5, Qwen3-Omni 10.6 / 0.5 / 4 / 0, Kimi-Audio-7B 19 / 79 / 11 / 5, Audio Flamingo 3 17.4 / 24.5 / 11.5 / 34, Step-Audio-R1 17.45 / 1.5 / 9.5 / 13. No model averages above 31% - [benchmarklist VoxParadox page](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/voxparadox/)
- InstructTTSEval (Huang, Qiu et al., arXiv 2506.16381, June 2025): three tasks (Acoustic-Parameter Specification, Descriptive-Style Directive, Role-Play), English and Chinese, 1k cases each (6k), judged by gemini-2.5-pro-preview-05-06. Human check: 50 samples per task and language, half matched and half mismatched, majority of three annotators. A search summary reads Table 4 as about 79% average agreement and about 71% on Role-Play, and the authors say agreement falls on the more subjective role-play task. Those two figures are a reading of a partly extracted table and should be checked - [InstructTTSEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16381v1)
- AEQ-Bench (Findings of ACL 2026) reports that omni-model judges diverge significantly from human perception on emotional prosody (from a search snippet, paper not read) - [AEQ-Bench PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1813.pdf)
- QualiSpeech (Tsinghua, arXiv 2503.20290, 2025): 11 aspects of low-level speech quality with natural-language reasoning, 10,558 training items half synthetic, and the authors report existing auditory LLMs struggle to assess quality. It targets noise and distortion, and I could not confirm prosody coverage - [QualiSpeech](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20290v3)
- Step-Audio 2 technical report (arXiv 2507.16632, July 2025), self-reported StepEval-Audio-Paralinguistic averages: Step-Audio 2 mini 80.00, Kimi-Audio 49.64, GPT-4o Audio 43.45. Emotion: Step-Audio 2 mini 82, Step-Audio 2 86, Kimi-Audio 66. The dataset card gives different Step-Audio 2 figures (average 76.55, emotion 72) - [Step-Audio 2 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16632), [StepEval dataset card mirror](https://hface.pages.dev/datasets/stepfun-ai/StepEval-Audio-Paralinguistic)

## 4. How is delivery checked against an acting direction?

### Takeaway
There is no reliable automatic check of a free-text direction like "whispering, afraid" against one take. Categorical emotion recognizers (emotion2vec+ large, 300M) agree with listeners only loosely on synthetic speech and disagree per emotion in both directions. Dimensional arousal, valence and dominance regression (audEERING, 0.2B, ONNX) is the most robust primitive for coarse checks such as calm versus agitated. Style-text embedding models (ParaSpeechCLAP, March 2026) give small but real gains when used to pick the best of 10 takes. Gemini as an instruction-following judge agrees with humans about 71 to 79% of the time on its benchmark.

### Cited findings

### Inferences
- A direction can be split by Claude, at script time, into checkable parts: an emotion class (maps to emotion2vec's 9 labels), an arousal level (whispering, calm, shouting), a pace word (slowly, rushed) and a free-text remainder. Each part then has a cheap acoustic check: class probability, arousal value, syllables per second from the ASR timestamps. The free-text remainder is left to ParaSpeechCLAP similarity or not checked.
- Whispering is a voice quality, not an emotion. It is better detected by low arousal plus a voicing or periodicity measure from the pitch tracker than by an emotion classifier.
- Because emotion2vec over-calls happy and under-calls sad on TTS compared with listeners, use it as a veto only when the take's top class directly contradicts the direction with high probability (for example "angry" direction and "happy" top class), not as a positive pass.
- Both the audEERING model and WhiStress are non-commercial licences, fine for a personal pipeline.

- No standard metric for "does this take follow this free-text direction" with per-sample human agreement above about 80% was found.

## 5. Other prosody defects worth checking, and which pitch trackers to use

### Takeaway
Questions, monotone delivery, rate jumps, a character's pitch drifting across takes and odd pauses can all be measured with a pitch track plus the word timestamps Parakeet already produces, but I found no published validation of such rules on TTS output. They are best used as relative checks within a line's takes or within a character's history. For pitch on CPU, SwiftF0 (95,842 parameters, about 133 ms per 5 s of audio) is the most accurate in its author's benchmark, Praat and RAPT are far faster at slightly lower accuracy, and librosa's pYIN (already installed) is accurate but about 10 times slower than SwiftF0.

### Cited findings
- A phrase-final F0 rise is the common cross-language cue for perceiving intonation questions, and the L* H-H% rise has been proposed for identifying typical questions - [Liscombe et al. 2006](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~julia/papers/liscombe_etal_2006.pdf), [perception of intonation questions](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-perception-of-intonation-questions-and-statements-in-1gmihh567c.pdf)
- In automatic question classification, Shriberg et al. found duration and pausing more predictive than pitch (as reported by Liscombe et al.) - [Liscombe et al. 2006](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~julia/papers/liscombe_etal_2006.pdf)
- Listener preference for synthesized questions depended more on overall voice quality than on intonation (Syrdal and Jilka) - [Syrdal and Jilka 2004](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~julia/papers/syrdal&jilka04.pdf)
- An older statistical TTS prosody model needed an explicit yes-no question feature to get question prosody right - [Edinburgh ICSLP 2002](https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/downloads/publications/2002/paper.icslp02.pdf)
- EmergentTTS-Eval has a Questions category, and question items showed weaker depth-related differences between systems than other categories - [EmergentTTS-Eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23009v1)
- Surprise delivery carries a final pitch rise that listeners heard as sentence-final emphasis - [EME-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12015v1)
- SwiftF0 (arXiv 2508.18440, 25 Aug 2025, code github.com/lars76/swift-f0): 95,842 parameters, range 46.875 to 2093.75 Hz. Harmonic-mean accuracy averaged over Bach10-mf0-synth, Vocadito and SpeechSynth (clean / 10 dB SNR) and CPU time per 5 s of audio: SwiftF0 94.07 / 91.80, 132.6 ms. PENN 89.23 / 59.87, 919.0 ms. Praat 90.13 / 76.10, 7.0 ms. pYIN 85.27 / 79.99, 1420.6 ms. RAPT 82.53 / 74.70, 13.6 ms. SWIPE 81.33 / 71.43, 133.3 ms. CREPE (about 22M) 80.70 / 78.97, 5508.3 ms. YAAPT 78.80 / 65.30. BasicPitch 31.17. These are the author's own benchmark and metric, the average is not speech-only, and RMVPE, FCPE, pyworld Harvest and SPICE were not compared - [SwiftF0](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18440v1)
- An FCPE paper reports 96.79% raw pitch accuracy on a dataset I could not identify, not comparable to the SwiftF0 numbers - [emergentmind SwiftF0 topic](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/swiftf0)

### Inferences
- Question check: only yes-no questions (not wh-questions, which normally fall in English) should be expected to rise. Claude can tag the intended question type in the script. A take whose last voiced 300 to 500 ms falls while the line is tagged as a rising yes-no question can be retaken, preferring the take with the largest final rise in semitones. Expressive readers sometimes fall on yes-no questions, so this is a preference among takes, not a hard fail.
- Monotone or flat take: the pitch range (for example the 5th to 95th percentile of F0 in semitones) of the take compared with the same character's typical range from earlier accepted takes. A take far below the character's norm when the direction is not "flat" or "deadpan" gets retaken.
- Rate jumps: syllables or words per second from the Parakeet timestamps, compared with the character's median and with the neighbouring lines. A direction that says "slowly" or "rushed" sets the expected side.
- Pitch-range drift of a character across takes: track the median F0 and range per character per chapter. A cast voice drifting over a chapter is a seed or reference-clip issue, and a z-score per take against the character's history catches it cheaply.
- Pause placement: gaps between Parakeet word timestamps longer than a threshold that do not fall at punctuation or a clause boundary are candidates for retake. Breeze's text input with punctuation is the main lever here.
- For the audio Python (numpy, scipy, librosa), librosa.pyin is available now but slow (about 1.4 s per 5 s in the SwiftF0 benchmark). SwiftF0 is about 10 times faster and more accurate in that benchmark, and Praat-class trackers (RAPT) are faster still, which matters when every take of about 220 lines is checked.

### Gaps
- No published evaluation of automatic question-rise, monotony, rate or pause rules on TTS output, and no thresholds tuned for audiobook speech, were found.
- No head-to-head accuracy or CPU timing for RMVPE, FCPE or pyworld Harvest against SwiftF0 on speech.
- SwiftF0's licence and whether it ships as ONNX were not confirmed.
- No study of character pitch drift across many zero-shot cloned takes was found.

## 6. Which fixes work, and what Breeze TTS 2 supports

### Takeaway
The fix with the best evidence is to take the line again with a new seed and rerank several takes with an automatic scorer (stress, style, naturalness). Every best-of-N study found gains, with the caveat that the output drifts toward the scorer's biases. Text-side levers are weak: in CAST, explicit emphasis markup on the best systems lifted the hit rate only to about 52%, and natural-language instructions and added context gave no clear gain. Breeze TTS 2 documents no emphasis markup at all. It offers text, a free-text instruction (direction), cfg_scale, seed, temperature and top-k, inline vocal-event tags in parentheses, and parallel streams, but no scores or logprobs.

### Cited findings
Breeze TTS 2
- Breeze TTS 2 (BreezeBlue): 3B parameters, weights and PyTorch inference released 2026-08-25. The audio tokenizer is based on Qwen3-TTS. Weights and self-hosted outputs are under the BreezeBlue Research and Non-Commercial License, code Apache 2.0 - [Breeze TTS 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2), [breeze-tts GitHub](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts)
- Delivery controls: Voice Direction is reference audio plus `--instruction`, "steering tone, emotion, pace, and delivery", for example "Speak slowly with a restrained, serious tone." Adding `--instruction` switches Voice Clone to Voice Direction. `--cfg-scale 4` is recommended "to strengthen instruction-following", and cfg_scale=1 is plain cloning. A repetition penalty exists. Vocal events go inline in parentheses in English, such as (laugh), (cough), (clears throat), (sigh). The docs say nothing about capitals, asterisks, italics, SSML or punctuation effects, and give no instruction-following metrics - [breeze-tts GitHub](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts), [Breeze TTS 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2)
- Breeze-TTS-2.cpp (HoppouAI, ggml, Vulkan or CPU, Apache 2.0 source): GGUF from F16 (5.9 GB) to q4_k (2.4 GB), q8_0 recommended. breeze-server documents `POST /v1/audio/speech` with text, instruction and voice_id (send with `--form-string` so text starting with "(" is not parsed as a group), also OpenAI-style JSON, output 24 kHz 16-bit PCM or MP3, WAV, PCM. The CLI and C API expose `--cfg-scale`, `--temp`, `--top-k` and `req.seed`, and the README does not say which of those HTTP accepts. cfg_scale 2 to 3 is suggested for vocal events to fire. `--parallel N` runs N streams (five q8_0 streams at 1.46x to 1.68x real time on an RTX 3080), and HTTP returns 409 when slots are full. No scores, logprobs or multiple-candidate API are documented - [Breeze-TTS-2.cpp GitHub](https://github.com/HoppouAI/Breeze-TTS-2.cpp), [GGUF model card](https://huggingface.co/HoppouAI/Breeze-TTS-2.cpp)

- EmphTTS (Aalto, arXiv 2609.27599 v2, 6 Oct 2026, code aalto-speech/EmphTTS): in pilot tests asterisks around the stressed word worked best for Qwen3-TTS-Voice-Design and VoxCPM2. Vendor guidance for its own engine, not a study - [Inworld prompting for TTS](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/best-practices/prompting-for-tts)

Best-of-N and reranking
- SpeechJudge-GRM picked the best of 100 Qwen2.5-Omni-7B Talker outputs on hard prompts, and human raters preferred the picks over random samples, more so than with the Bradley-Terry reward. As an online DPO reward it raised a 0.5B TTS model's N-CMOS to 0.25 versus 0.16 for offline DPO - [SpeechJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07931v2)
- ParaSpeechCLAP best-of-10 raised style consistency and tag recall slightly with no naturalness loss (numbers in section 4) - [ParaSpeechCLAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28737v1)
- LLaSA (arXiv 2502.04128) used speech-understanding models as verifiers at inference and found more inference compute improved emotional expressiveness, timbre consistency and content accuracy, but "scaling inference-time compute shifts the sampling modes toward the biases of specific verifiers" - [LLaSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128v2)
- A LAION research log (not peer reviewed) reports that best-of-N with a blended WER and learned-quality reward captured 73% of the gain at N=25 and 90% at N=50 for expressive TTS - [LAION research log](https://projects.laion.ai/laion-moss-local-1.5-voice-acting-4.55b/overview.html)

Signal-side prosody editing
- The only data point found: WhiStress made detectable stress by synthesizing stressed words slower, louder and +1.5 semitones through SSML, and the result sounded robotic until hand-tuned - [WhiStress paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)

### Inferences
- A plausible automatic loop for Breeze, in order of evidence:
  1. At script time Claude tags narrow-focus lines with the focus word, acceptable alternatives and competitor words (section 2), the question type, and a split direction (emotion class, arousal, pace).
  2. Render the take as today. Parakeet checks the words. For tagged lines only, WhiStress checks the focus word, the pitch track checks the question rise, and emotion2vec or arousal veto a clear contradiction of the direction.
  3. On a failure, render 2 to 4 more seeds and keep the take with the best combined score (words first, then stress, then direction). `--parallel` lets breeze-server produce these concurrently if VRAM allows.
  4. If no seed passes, rewrite the line rather than mark it up: Claude can restructure the sentence so the default accent lands on the focus word (end-focus, a cleft such as "It was Marek who...", moving the word to the end, or splitting the sentence), or move the emphasis into the direction ("stressing the word 'him'"). CAST suggests instructions alone rarely help, so the sentence rewrite is the stronger text lever, but it has not been tested.
  5. After a bounded number of attempts, accept the best take and log it, since AJ cannot review on a screen.
- Back-of-envelope on retakes: CAST shows a single TTS take hits a context-required stress target about 30 to 42% of the time. If seeds were independent at that rate, 4 takes would give at least one hit about 76 to 89% of the time. Seed independence is an untested assumption, and the detector's own noise (kappa 0.32) means some "hits" are wrong.
- Asterisks or capitals with Breeze are untested. Breeze's codec derives from Qwen3-TTS, but the text side and backbone are Breeze's own, and its docs never mention markup. A small A/B on a scratch serial (same line, same seed, plain versus asterisks versus capitals, scored by WhiStress) would settle it. A literal asterisk risks being read aloud or changing the take in other ways.
- Because a cfg_scale of 4 is recommended to make Breeze follow instructions, the direction's effect on prosody may depend strongly on cfg_scale. Raising it for lines whose direction check fails is a cheap knob to test.
- Post-hoc prosody editing (pitch-shift and lengthen one word with WORLD or PSOLA at the Parakeet timestamps) is feasible with the audio Python, but there is no evidence it sounds natural on neural TTS, and the WhiStress experience suggests rule-based boosts sound robotic. Keep it last, if at all.
- Every reranker pulls takes toward its own bias (LLaSA). Combining unrelated scorers (word accuracy, stress, arousal) and capping N limits that.

### Gaps
- No documentation or test of how Breeze TTS 2 treats capitals, asterisks, italics, quotes or ellipses.
- Which sampling fields breeze-server's HTTP endpoint accepts (seed, temp, top_k, cfg_scale) is not stated in its README.
- No study measures how often a new seed fixes a misplaced stress, or how correlated takes are across seeds at temperature 0.9.
- No evaluation of post-hoc emphasis editing on neural TTS output was found.
- No study of sentence rewriting (clefts, word order) as a fix for TTS stress errors was found.
