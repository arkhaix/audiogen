# Turn take checks into automatic fixes

As of November 2026 nobody checks a plain-text TTS take much better than audiogen already does: transcribe it, diff it against the line, and roll a new seed when it fails. Every open-source audiobook tool that closes the loop does the same with a looser string ratio, commercial proofing does the same and hands the list to a person, and the research systems that do better read TTS internals that Breeze TTS 2 does not expose. The gains left for audiogen come less from new models than from evidence the pipeline already computes and throws away: substitutions, which never flag a take although **192 of the 296 substituted script words in the retired copy were bible names**, Parakeet's per-token timestamps, each take's speech level, the pauses the join trims, and the author's italics. Three small CPU models add the rest: a voice activity detector, a speaker embedding and a phone recognizer for name spans. Of the five defects AJ listed, silences and odd volume changes reduce to signal measurements that can end in a repair or a retake now. Names fit the pick-one flow AJ already uses for voices and has accepted for pronunciation: the pipeline notices a new or unstable name, renders a few candidate respellings, files one automatically, and he picks on a page. The automatic work left is holding every later take to the pick, a check no benchmark yet covers on TTS output. **The wrong word emphasized cannot yet be checked reliably enough to act on without a person**: the best detector agrees with listeners at kappa 0.32 where listeners agree with each other at 0.36, so it can only choose among takes. Some targets beat items on his list: endings that fade or get cut off, long lines that lose half their words, a take that drifts from its voice's timbre or level, clipping copied from a cast clip, and text that Breeze should never see raw. The one bad take AJ named, in chapter 33 of The Hundred Reigns, turned out to be a take the existing check had flagged and nothing replaced, and **nine more flagged takes sit in older chapters**, so the step before all others is to take again what the pipeline already knows is wrong. After that, the build order is to wire the measurements already in hand into the one place where a retake is cheap and add the pronunciation pick, which needs no new model, then add the three CPU models, which also screen pronunciation candidates and hold takes to the pick, and to leave prosody scorers for last, as rankers. No figure in the sources was measured on Breeze output in English, so every threshold below is a starting point to tune on his own takes.

*Facts carry a source: a link for outside work, a `file:line` for audiogen's own code. A sentence that gives a researcher's proposal or this report's own inference says so.*

## The field still checks takes the way audiogen already does

Published TTS benchmarks measure test sets, not takes. Seed-TTS-eval reports one word error rate from Whisper-large-v3 and one speaker similarity from a WavLM-large verifier over its whole English set ([Seed-TTS-eval](https://github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval)). Pass or fail rules for one take come instead from 2024 to 2026 papers that filter training data. AlignDPO counts a candidate clean only on an exact word match, with **one edit tolerated under ten words and any deletion failing it**, and keeps a pair only when two independent recognizers agree ([AlignDPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12855v1)). "Taming Long-form Text-to-Speech" defines a **skip as 10 or more contiguous deleted words and a hallucination as 20 or more contiguous substituted or inserted words** ([Taming long-form TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16989v1)). Forced alignment of the script is the strongest published detector, and it works as a trigger, not a verdict: AlignDPO's detector over Parakeet-CTC-1.1B caught about **95% of skipped, repeated or fabricated words at 49% precision**, because it over-flags rare words ([AlignDPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12855v1)). In a fantasy serial the rare words are the names.

Working systems close the loop the same way, only more loosely. The open-source audiobook tools that check at all transcribe with Whisper, score a fuzzy string ratio and retry with a new seed. Chatterbox-TTS-Extended renders 3 candidates with up to 3 attempts each, passes one at a difflib ratio of 0.85 (0.95 on retries), keeps the **shortest passing take**, and falls back to the longest transcript when all fail ([Chatter.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petermg/Chatterbox-TTS-Extended/main/Chatter.py)). epub2tts makes two attempts at a fuzz ratio of 88 ([epub2tts.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aedocw/epub2tts/main/epub2tts.py)). AllTalk's check is advisory ([AllTalk wiki](https://github.com/erew123/alltalk_tts/wiki/TTS-Generator)), and a practitioner's VoxCPM2 gate went warn-only after it rejected 4 of 8 shorts its author judged fine, mostly over proper nouns and numbers ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/voxcpm2-news-shorts-narration-2080ti)). The largest projects, among them ebook2audiobook, audiblez and abogen, document no recognizer check ([ebook2audiobook](https://github.com/DrewThomasson/ebook2audiobook), [audiblez](https://github.com/santinic/audiblez), [abogen](https://github.com/denizsafak/abogen)). Commercial proofing from Pozotron, Penguin Random House's Orion QC and an Amazon patent diffs a transcript against the manuscript, flags missed, added, repeated and misread words and long pauses (Orion flags silences over 3 s), and gives the list to a person ([Pozotron](https://blog.pozotron.com/what-is-the-pozotron-proofing-tool), [Orion QC](https://tech.bertelsmann.com/en/blog/articles/orion-qc), [US10922484](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10922484)). The only public commercial loop that fixes without a person is ElevenLabs Studio's Auto-Regenerate, which regenerates up to two times when it detects mispronunciations or artefacts, by a method that is not public, and whose help page was read only through a search snippet ([ElevenLabs help](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/30204568681745-What-is-Auto-Regenerate)). The systems researcher concludes, and this report agrees, that audiogen's loop of a length guard, a Parakeet diff and two rounds of new seeds before the join is already stricter and more automatic than any open-source audiobook tool found.

The research frontier mostly needs controls Breeze lacks. The long-form paper reads the TTS model's own alignment heads, rolls back to the onset of a failure and reseeds under an attention mask, which cut worst-of-10-seed WER at 1,500 words or more from 35.2% to 3.4% ([Taming long-form TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16989v1)). Vox-Evaluator locates the bad segment and regenerates only that span, which cut VoiceCraft's WER from 7.56 to 5.11, and has no code ([Vox-Evaluator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20210)). GRAFT conditions a codec TTS on a spoken sample of a word, also with no code ([GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02633)). Breeze TTS 2 takes text, an instruction, a voice and sampling settings, and documents **no phoneme input, no emphasis markup, no segment infill and no scores or log-probabilities** ([Breeze-TTS-2.cpp](https://github.com/HoppouAI/Breeze-TTS-2.cpp), [Breeze TTS 2 card](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2)). For a plain-text TTS with a seed, the usable state of the art comes down to four moves: detect, take again with a new seed, choose among a few takes with a scorer, and change the text when seeds keep failing.

Each move has a measured ceiling. Best-of-N helps modestly: on F5-TTS the best single recognizer verifier lowered WER from **2.06% to 1.72% at N=10**, a rank ensemble across recognizer families reached 1.61%, and a verifier from the same family as the judge recovered 2 to 3 times more of the headroom than a cross-family one, which inflates any gain measured by the recognizer that chose the take ([Yu and Kang, ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08256)). Choosing on speaker similarity alone made Llasa's WER worse, and every verifier pulls takes toward its own biases ([Llasa](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128v2)). Learned judges rank systems well and single takes poorly. UTMOSv2 reaches a system-level SRCC of 0.919 but 0.613 per utterance ([UTMOSv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09305v1)). The best audio-language model matched a binary naturalness preference 69.1% of the time, where human raters agree with each other 69.0% ([SpeechJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07931v2)). On VoxParadox, where the voice contradicts the words, **no model averages above 31%**, because the models answer from the transcript ([VoxParadox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27772), [scores](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/voxparadox/)). Claude accepts no audio input ([Claude features](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/overview.md)), so Claude's place in the loop is the text: deciding what right should sound like before the voice speaks.

## The pipeline discards most of the evidence a better check needs

audiogen already runs every move except the last. A cpp take passes a length guard of 0.12 x w to 2.25 + 0.75 x w seconds for w words, with up to three seeds (`serial_audio/breeze.py:59-75`). While breeze-server still holds the model, Parakeet transcribes the new takes on the CPU, and a take with **three or more inserted or three or more dropped words** is `flagged` and taken again for up to two more rounds of three tries, at most nine generations per line, with a new take kept only when it is strictly less bad (`breeze.py:454-517`, `takecheck.py:47-66`). A word error rate over 0.34 makes a take only `suspect`, and suspect and unreadable takes are never taken again (`breeze.py:485`). The join fills sub-millisecond clicks from an autoregressive model, trims edges, gives each voice one gain toward -18 dB of median active-speech RMS, puts 0.3 s of digital silence between takes, cuts every silent run to 0.75 s, and bends samples toward 0.99 of full scale before an Ogg Opus encode at soundfile's default bitrate (`breeze_join.py:72-120`, `fx.py:26-30`, `fx.py:80-89`, `fx.py:116-122`). On what it catches the loop works. The live library flagged **17 of 7,602 takes in 34 chapters**, and in a scratch copy the first new take replaced each of eight flagged takes (`DECISIONS.md:43-46`). The one bad take AJ named, The Hundred Reigns chapter 33 at 8:55 to 9:05, did not slip past this check. The check flagged it, and the take stayed in the audio for a reason the section after this one gives.

What it throws away is the material for the checks AJ asked about. A substitution never flags a take, however many there are (`takecheck.py:59-62`). sherpa-onnx documents per-token timestamps for this recognizer and durations for TDT models ([sherpa-onnx result](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/c-api/html/structsherpa__onnx_1_1cxx_1_1OfflineRecognizerResult.html)), but `parakeet_asr.py` reads only the text (`parakeet_asr.py:69`), so no word timing exists anywhere in the pipeline. The join computes each take's active-speech level for the voice gain and drops it (`breeze_join.py:106`), keeps clicks only as a chapter total (`breeze_join.py:94`), records nothing about the pauses it trims (`fx.py:239-268`), and logs the voice gains without storing them (`breeze.py:790-798`). The clipping test that marks cast candidates, three or more full-scale samples in a row, never runs on takes (`casting.py:320-343`), although Breeze copies a clipped clip's distortion into every take, and the Keeper's chapter 11 lines sounded clipped after the join leveled them (`docs/casting.md:33`). The character pass writes a `respelling` with the stressed syllable in capitals for each term, and no code uses it (`render.py:132-146`). Author emphasis dies twice: the Royal Road parser turns `<em>` and `<i>` into asterisks on 8 to 34 lines of each Hundred Reigns chapter, the screenplay pass drops every asterisk, and the EPUB parser keeps no emphasis at all (`royalroad.py:208-215`, `prompts/screenplay_pass.system.md:14`, `epub.py:6`). The script schema holds only speaker, text and direction (`schemas/screenplay_pass.schema.json:9-15`), so there is nowhere to put a focus word, a heteronym sense or an intended pronunciation.

The only measured take data points at names and short lines. In the retired library copy, 824 takes of chapters 7 to 10 of The Hundred Reigns were checked on 2026-11-07 by Qwen3-ASR, not by Parakeet. 27 came out suspect, 1 unreadable and none flagged. **21 of the 27 suspects were a name heard another way**, 3 were one-word lines with an empty transcript ("Oh...", "Thalas?", "Hey!"), 2 were invented compounds split in two, and the unreadable take was "The kish?" transcribed in Devanagari. Across all 824 takes, Thalas (55), Duchar (27) and Casval (21) led the substituted names, and Casval came back as kazval, caswell, kaysval and caswall, a spread the survey could not place in the audio or in the recognizer. Speech ran at a median 0.4 s per word (5th to 95th percentile 0.3 to 0.5), so the length guard's bounds sit far from normal speech on both sides and catch only gross failures. The Hundred Reigns scripts have a median line of 9 words, but 72 of 2,651 lines run over 60 words, and the two flagged lines of 58 and 64 words had lost 25 and 30 words (`DECISIONS.md:46`). These figures come from the survey of audiogen's code and retired copy in `research_notes/TTS take quality checks/current_pipeline.md`, section 8.

One fact about the pipeline's order decides where new checks belong. The only point where a finding can roll a new seed for the cost of one take, about **2.8 s on the laptop and 17.5 s on the Mac** (`DECISIONS.md:115`), is the check before the join, while breeze-server is still up (`breeze.py:746-758`). By the time the join measures anything, breeze-server has stopped (`breeze.py:759-768`), and acting on a finding there means starting it again: a 2.0 s warm-up on the laptop (`library/the-hundred-reigns/chapters/0007/breeze_server.log:6`) but about 40 s of Metal shader compiling on the Mac (`docs/machines.md:81`). Parakeet already runs in a subprocess under the audio Python, which has numpy, scipy, librosa and sherpa-onnx on both nodes (`asr.py:121`, `docs/machines.md:62`, `docs/machines.md:85`). This report infers from that layout that signal measurements should ride in the same subprocess before the join, and that every new verdict needs a rank in `VERDICTS` and `badness` and a pass through the filter at `breeze.py:485`, or it will be reported and never fixed.

## The take AJ named was flagged and never replaced

*This section was added after the rest of the report. It comes from reading the live library on containers and the chapter's takes on the laptop on 2026-11-12. Nothing was changed or rendered.*

The take at 8:55 to 9:05 of chapter 33 is script line 72, a narrator line of 20 words: "Eole replied as Agnes returned with the books. She then addressed the elf in a tongue that Simon didn't recognize." Rebuilding the join's timeline from the chapter's 189 takes gives 1,341.30 s, the length of the joined file, and puts line 72 at **8:54.50 to 9:05.10**. Parakeet heard "Olchreis great, maybe diffilly replied, The lock's resistance for missiturabit. He had dumb insisted to undress the elf in his prime and shoe." The check counted 14 substituted, 2 dropped and 5 inserted words and marked the take `flagged` (`take_check.json` of that chapter). The signal agrees with the transcript. The take runs at 0.54 s per word where the narrator's neighbouring lines run 0.37 to 0.41, it holds four silences of 0.4 to 1.0 s where the line has one full stop, and its level dips 23 to 42 dB below its own speech level four times.

So the check worked and the fix did not exist yet. Chapter 33 was rendered at 09:56 on 2026-11-11 and checked two minutes later, after its join. The retake before the join was committed at 13:39 that day, and its files on containers are dated 18:32. Until then a flagged take was only listed on the chapter page for AJ to hear.

Nothing fixes it now either. A render reuses a saved take when its key matches, its file exists and its length is in bounds, whatever the take check said about it (`serial_audio/breeze.py:677-685`), and the retake loop checks only the takes that render has just made (`breeze.py:747-758`, `DECISIONS.md:46`). A new render of chapter 33 would join the same take again.

**The library holds ten such takes.** On 2026-11-12 the 42 checked chapters of The Hundred Reigns held 9,383 takes: 9,151 ok, 221 suspect, 1 unreadable and 10 flagged, and none of the ten had been taken again. They are chapter 13 line 44, chapter 16 line 207, chapter 19 line 235, chapter 22 line 67, chapter 24 line 236, chapter 26 line 117, chapter 27 lines 178 and 236, chapter 30 line 62 and chapter 33 line 72, all rendered between 2026-11-09 and the morning of 2026-11-11. The lines in chapters 16 and 19 are the 64- and 58-word lines that lost 30 and 25 words. Four chapters rendered since then (38, 42, 45 and 46) list retaken lines, so the loop works on new renders. DECISIONS counted 17 flagged takes in 7,602 on 2026-11-11, and this examination did not trace the difference.

The fix is small, and it is this report's proposal: let the retake loop cover a saved take whose stored verdict, for the same audio, is `flagged`. In the scratch test of 2026-11-11 the first new take replaced each of eight flagged takes (`DECISIONS.md:46`). It replaces the audio of nine chapters. AJ said yes to it on 2026-11-12. The change was written that day and passed a scratch render of chapter 33, where the new take of line 72 reads the line but for the name Eole. It was deployed later that day, the nine chapters were rendered, and the library then held no flagged take.

The same passage also bears on two rules proposed below. First, line 72 was flagged only because it also had five inserted words. With two it would have been `suspect` and stayed in every later render, since substitutions never flag a take. Across the 9,383 takes only 3 unflagged takes have three or more substituted words that make up 30% or more of the line, so a rule that flags heavy substitution would cost a handful of retakes in 42 chapters. Second, within the half minute around it Parakeet wrote Belzemine as "Belzemine", "Basmin", "Belzamine" and "Expezamine", and Eole as "Aholy" and "Eol". Line 85, "Ag, Belzemine nodded and then answered Eole in Elven.", came back as "Expezamine nodded, and then answered Eol and Elvin.", with 5 wrong words of 9 and a verdict of `suspect`. Four of the five are words of the story's own. The transcripts cannot say whether the voice or the recognizer varied, which is the case for a phone check on name spans (option 13) and against flagging on substitutions where the substituted words are names.

## Silences, volume and name drift yield to automatic fixes, emphasis does not

The five listed defects differ less in how hard they are to detect than in whether anyone can write down the right answer in advance. A silence can be judged against a punctuation mark and a level against a voice's median, so both reduce to arithmetic. A name's consistency can be judged against its own earlier occurrences. A name's correctness and a sentence's focus need a target someone chose. For names AJ will choose, from rendered candidates, the way he chooses voices. For focus, a machine can propose the target, but no check can yet hold a take to it.

### Silences inside a take need word timings the pipeline already computes

No check looks at silence inside a take: the join cuts every silent run to 0.75 s and records nothing (`fx.py:239-268`). Published norms make an unexplained pause easy to define. In 80 read-aloud passages, speakers paused at **94.2% of full stops (mean 734 ms), 69.4% of clause boundaries (652 ms), 44.7% of commas (511 ms) and only 5.7% of unpunctuated phrase boundaries (471 ms)** ([Hunt 2023](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/10304)). The join's 0.75 s cap therefore matches the full-stop mean, and a mid-line gap under 0.75 s with no punctuation passes it untouched. The content researcher proposes flagging any gap over 0.5 to 0.6 s between two words with no punctuation between them, and repairing it in the signal by shortening it to about 150 to 250 ms when the words on both sides are intact, with a retake otherwise. That needs word timings. In sherpa-onnx's example, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 returns per-token timestamps on an 80 ms grid ([sherpa-onnx NeMo models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/pretrained_models/offline-transducer/nemo-transducer-models.html)), too coarse for phone boundaries but fine for pauses of hundreds of milliseconds. No accuracy figure for those timestamps is published, and whether the sherpa-onnx 2.0.0 on the laptop exposes them for this model is unverified. If they fall short, forced alignment of the script is the fallback: on human speech MFA 3.x lands about 20 ms from word boundaries, MMS 43 to 50 ms, NeMo 78 to 89 ms and WhisperX about 110 ms ([MFA 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18466)).

The same timings expose the rest of the timing family. Audio that continues after the last aligned word is a breath, a hum or babble, and the researchers propose trimming at the last word plus 100 to 200 ms when no extra words are heard there, and treating extra words as an insertion that earns a retake. Silero VAD 6.x (about 2 MB, under 1 ms per 32 ms chunk on one CPU thread) and TEN VAD (real-time factor 0.016 on an M1) both run through the sherpa-onnx already installed ([Silero VAD](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad), [TEN VAD](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad)). Speaking rate and drawn-out words can be compared with each voice's own distribution by interquartile fences, the outlier rule the Emilia data pipeline applies to character durations ([Emilia](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05361v3)), with a retake as the fix. The same per-voice rate would let the length guard tighten from bounds that sit far from normal speech to bounds that fit each voice (this report's inference). Between takes, the fixed 0.3 s sits at the short end of read-speech sentence pauses (about 200 to 1,360 ms) and below paragraph pauses (about 400 to 1,800 ms), from a study read only in abstract ([CEEOL](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=871661)), and one audiobook tool uses 500 ms between speakers and 250 ms within one ([Alexandria](https://github.com/Finrandojin/alexandria-audiobook)). A pause chosen from the script (same speaker, speaker change, paragraph end, scene break) is a post-processing fix that needs no check at all, though no audiobook norm for those lengths was found.

### Odd volume changes are three problems, and the join sees one

The per-voice gain fixed the large problem AJ heard, takes that come out at their cast clip's level anywhere from -11 to -29 dB (`DECISIONS.md:141-144`). One gain per voice per chapter still leaves a single loud or quiet take where it landed, and Breeze users report exactly that: the same reference and seed gave different volume and timbre across segments, and novel narration showed volume and speed fluctuating between segments ([breeze-tts issue 25](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/25)). The take's level is already computed in the join, so a per-take gain toward the voice's target costs little. The signal researcher proposes keeping intent by offsetting each take's target from its direction, whisper -6 LU, soft -3, normal 0, raised +2 and shout +3, clamped to 6 dB either way, so a whisper stays a whisper while a whisper that came out at full level is pulled down. Those offsets are a proposal to set by listening, not a published value.

A swing inside one take has no published detector. The researcher's proposal is momentary loudness every 100 ms over active frames, flagging a 95th-to-10th-percentile spread above about 8 to 10 LU or a jump of more than about 6 LU between neighbouring 400 ms windows, with thresholds to calibrate. One Breeze swing is on record. In a 26-seed Mandarin sweep, one seed's final sentence faded as if the speaker walked away, with the 300 to 3,000 Hz band **down 7.5 dB against the body**, and maintainers blamed the GPU's missing BF16 and a reference transcript that did not match its audio ([breeze-tts issue 14](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/14)). Comparing a take's last second, band by band, with the rest of it would catch that and send the line to a retake. Clipping belongs here too: running the cast candidates' full-scale test on every take, before leveling makes it audible, ends in a retake or in a fill by autoregressive interpolation, the method the declicker already uses and that ffmpeg's adeclip applies to clipped samples ([FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)).

Between voices the meter itself is the issue. BS.1770 loudness applies K-weighting, a high-pass near 38 Hz and a shelf of about +4 dB above roughly 1.5 kHz, and gates silence ([EBU Tech 3342](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3342.pdf), [K-weighting](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/glossary/terms-audio/k-weighting)). The signal researcher infers from the filter shape that at equal RMS a deep voice measures and sounds quieter than a bright one, so leveling voices on K-weighted active-speech power instead of RMS fixes the gap with the same code. No source quantifies that gap for speech. Integrated LUFS on a 1 to 3 s take rests on a few 400 ms blocks, and loudness range is not defined for programmes under a minute, so per-take measures should use K-weighted power over active frames and leave loudness range to the chapter ([EBU Tech 3342](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3342.pdf)). Speech delivery targets run from **-18 LUFS** (AES TD1008) to -16 LKFS with a **-1 dBTP** true-peak ceiling (Apple) ([TD1008](https://productionadvice.co.uk/td1008/), [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/audio-requirements)), and the current soft limit, bending toward 0.99 of full scale or about -0.1 dBFS by arithmetic, does not hold that ceiling. No standard gives a loudness range for a car or for falling asleep. Auphonic puts news near 3 LU and talks near 5 LU and says commuter shows want less still ([Auphonic](https://us.auphonic.com/blog/2019/08/19/dynamic-range-processing/)), and Dolby's Line mode, which holds dialogue steady while boosting quiet passages and cutting loud ones, is the model for bed listening ([Dolby DP569](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/76738/dolby-laboratories-dp569.html?page=65)). The researcher's synthesis, not a spec, is -18 LUFS measured as mono, -1 dBTP, a chapter range of about 4 to 6 LU, and a gentle 2:1 compressor above the normal level to cap shouts without touching ordinary lines.

Some of the spread can go before any take exists. BreezeBlue's hosted platform prepares each clone reference by denoising it, cutting an exactly transcribed excerpt of at most 30 s at a sentence or pause boundary, and normalizing it to **-18 LUFS** ([breeze-tts issue 25](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/25), [BreezeBlue voice clone guide](https://docs.breezeblue.ai/guides/voice-clone)). Because audiogen's takes come out at their clip's level, preparing new cast clips the same way would narrow the spread at its source. For an existing voice it would change the clip hash inside every take key (`breeze.py:186-187`) and so re-render its takes, which makes it a rule for new casts.

### Names said different ways can be held to AJ's pick and the name's own history

Parakeet cannot see this defect, for a structural reason. A transducer's prediction network acts as an internal language model ([Meng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01991)), so it writes the likeliest spelling: a near-miss of a known word comes out spelled right, and an invented name comes out in the recognizer's own spelling whether or not it was said right ([say-it-right write-up](https://dev.to/logitsolutionsai/speech-to-text-cant-tell-you-if-an-ai-voice-said-a-name-right-5bge)). Parakeet's card warns that rare words are "not likely to be recognized" ([Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2)). That is why so many name substitutions passed in the retired copy, and why four spellings of Casval prove nothing about the audio.

No paper or tool checks name consistency across TTS takes. Commercial practice is a pronunciation list plus listening, and Google Play Books plays every occurrence of a word for the publisher to hear ([Google Play Books](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/14182676), [Narration Box](https://narrationbox.com/blog/pronunciation-consistency-harder-than-voice-quality-audiobook-guide)). The pronunciation researcher assembles an automatic version from query-by-example spoken term detection, where phone posteriorgrams matched by dynamic time warping are the classic speaker-independent way to compare two spoken instances of a word ([MIT thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/79217/2/844753073-MIT.pdf)). The design takes one canonical rendering per name, which under the pick-one flow below is the candidate AJ picked, cuts the name's span from every take that holds it (from word timestamps or a CTC alignment of the line), decodes its phone string, and measures both a feature-weighted phone distance ([panphon](https://github.com/dmort27/panphon)) and a posteriorgram distance to the canonical. A take that is an outlier against that name's accepted history, for example above its 95th percentile, is taken again, keeping the take closest to the canonical. Clustering a name's occurrences across chapters also finds drift no single take shows, such as two voices that settled on two readings. Speaker independence matters because several cloned voices say each name, which in the researcher's judgment favours phone posteriorgrams over pooled self-supervised embeddings that keep more speaker information. No accuracy figure exists for any of this on synthetic voices, so the thresholds must come from AJ's own takes.

Two text levers sit beside the check. breeze-server accepts a per-request temperature (local Breeze-TTS-2.cpp v0.1.6 source, `docs\server.md`), so lines that carry a hard name could render cooler to narrow seed-to-seed variation, which is untested and risks flatter delivery. Biasing Parakeet toward the cast's names would quiet the name noise in the word check, and the VoxCPM2 practitioner's version, putting each line's names and numbers in Whisper's initial prompt, moved a 40-line golden set from 25 to 37 passes with no real misread let through ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/voxcpm2-news-shorts-narration-2080ti)). But sherpa-onnx hotwords need modified beam search on transducers, support for this Parakeet export is unconfirmed ([sherpa hotwords](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa/blob/master/docs/source/onnx/hotwords/index.rst)), and a word check biased toward a name hears the name it expects. This report's inference is that biasing is safe only after a phone check owns the names.

### A pronunciation pick modeled on casting settles what a name should sound like

The hard part is the target, and AJ has settled how to set it. LLM grapheme-to-phoneme conversion scores below human annotators in English ([PhonologyBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.02456v2.pdf)), and a 2026 benchmark names proper nouns as a major unmeasured error source ([G2P benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22009)), so Claude's IPA for an invented name is a proposal, not an answer. AJ has said a pick-one flow is acceptable for names, the same flow he uses for voices. Casting already has its shape: a casting pass writes a brief and a sample line, breeze-server designs several candidates, the best transcript match that is not clipped is filed with status `auto`, and on the Cast tab he plays every candidate and files one or recasts (`README.md:56-61`, `README.md:120-123`, `docs/casting.md:9-10`). For a name, the candidates are respellings. When the character pass adds a term, or a name proves unstable, Claude proposes 4 to 6 English respellings, none in parentheses, starting from the `respelling` the character pass already writes, together with an intended IPA and two or three likely misreadings. Breeze renders each candidate in a couple of real chapter lines, in the voices that will say the name, because casting learned that AJ heard Casval's reverb only in chapter lines and not in the sample clip (`docs/casting.md:20`). The candidates are brought to one level before he hears them, because casting also learned that a quiet clip sounds worse than it is (`docs/casting.md:31`). One candidate is filed as the say-as respelling with status `auto`, so no render waits for him, his pick on the page replaces it, and asking for new candidates is the recast.

Noticing needs no new model. A new name arrives as a new term in `bible/pronunciations.yaml` from the character pass, which never overwrites a term it already has (`passes.py:99-102`). An unstable name shows in data the take check already stores: the heard transcripts spelled Casval four wrong ways and Adrissant two in the retired copy, which can be recognizer noise, but is a cheap trigger for offering candidates, not a verdict on the audio. The phone recognizer earns its place in the next phase, in choosing which candidate to file and which to offer. Rendering each respelling with three seeds, 36 takes or about 100 s of laptop GPU per name for 6 respellings in 2 carriers by the pronunciation researcher's arithmetic, lets the recognizer score how close each respelling lands to Claude's intended IPA and how stable it is across seeds. The filed candidate is then the most stable close match, and a respelling that comes out several ways is never offered, since a pick means little if the voice will not repeat it. Seed-to-seed variation of Breeze on invented names is unmeasured, so the number of seeds is a guess. Without the recognizer the flow still works, with Claude's first respelling filed and AJ's ear doing the rest. Each automatic step has a precedent, from say-it-right's lexicon workflow to NVIDIA scoring heteronym candidates with an aligner, patents that add a pronunciation variant when recognition of a TTS voice fails, and Hitachi's LLM respellings for TTS input ([NVIDIA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14523), [US 8073694](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8073694), [Hitachi](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16920)), but nobody has published the whole loop or an accuracy figure for it. AJ's existing say-as decisions, Louis as "Lewis" and Lauriane as "Laurie Anne" (`DECISIONS.md:50-53`), are labelled examples to calibrate the scoring on. A pick for a name already heard in rendered chapters leaves those chapters with the old reading until they are rendered again, as a recast leaves earlier chapters with the earlier voice (`docs/casting.md:64-67`). After the pick, the automatic part is holding takes to it: the consistency check above uses the picked candidate as its canonical, scores each name span against it, and retakes the outliers.

Screening candidates and holding takes to a pick both need an ear without word priors. Universal phone recognizers improved sharply in 2025. ZIPA, Zipformer models trained on 17,132 hours, reaches a phonetic feature error rate of **about 0.7 to 2.2 on LibriSpeech English**, against 4.09 for wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft and 4.18 for Allosaurus in the same table, and its 64M small models ship as ONNX in FP32, FP16 and INT8 under CC BY 4.0, with the noisy-student variants non-commercial ([ZIPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23170v1), [ZIPA code](https://github.com/lingjzhu/zipa)). Two cautions travel with it. ZIPA's output matches dictionary pronunciations better than what speakers actually said, which its authors trace to G2P-derived training labels, and POWSM's audio-guided G2P got worse when given the text ([POWSM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24992v2)), so a check should decode phones freely from a CTC model with no text prompt. ZIPA's ONNX path still imports torch and lhotse, and no CPU speed is published for it.

The pronunciation researcher recommends scoring a name span two ways and accepting it only when both agree: a feature-weighted distance from the free phone decode to the intended IPA, and a CTC likelihood ratio of the intended pronunciation over its known misreadings, the goodness-of-pronunciation approach that Cao and colleagues made alignment-free for CTC models ([Cao et al.](https://papers.cool/venue/cao24b@interspeech_2024@ISCA), [GOP-SF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16838)). Once AJ has picked, the target for both scores is his picked rendering rather than Claude's IPA alone. The closest working tool is say-it-right, which runs three CPU ears on each take, Whisper small.en for words, a character CTC model with no language model, and wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak for phones, against a researched IPA per hard word. On its author's own 32 reads of 8 names, correct reads passing rose from 8 of 22 under a transcript check to 22 of 22, with 10 of 10 wrong reads caught, which the author calls "a fit, not a test". On 15,967 learner words its word-level ROC-AUC was 0.843 against 0.788 for plain phone error rate, and it checks a short take in 3 to 4 s on a laptop CPU with about 3.5 GB of models ([say-it-right](https://github.com/logitsolutionsai/say-it-right), [write-up](https://dev.to/logitsolutionsai/speech-to-text-cant-tell-you-if-an-ai-voice-said-a-name-right-5bge)). It was split out of another project on 2026-11-10 and had 5 commits and no tags, so its results are self-reported and days old.

Respelling is the only pronunciation lever Breeze offers. Its C++ tokenizer runs Gemma BPE with byte fallback on the raw text and normalizes nothing (local Breeze-TTS-2.cpp v0.1.6 source, `src\tokenizer.cpp`), so IPA typed into a line would arrive as byte tokens the model almost certainly never trained on, by the researcher's inference, and vendors call respellings fragile from one model to the next ([Picovoice](https://picovoice.ai/blog/custom-pronunciation-in-tts/)). The same candidates-and-scoring machinery covers heteronyms, with no pick needed, which a per-serial say-as map cannot do: the screenplay pass, which reads the whole chapter, can tag each read, lead, wind, bow or tear with its sense, the rarer sense can be respelled per occurrence, and the take can be verified with a two-way likelihood test between the two known readings. Per-homograph classifiers reach 0.991 on the public English homograph data ([Nicolis and Klimkov](https://www.isca-archive.org/ssw_2021/nicolis21_ssw.html)), but how often Breeze misreads heteronyms is unmeasured. Azure Pronunciation Assessment is a cloud alternative at about $1.32 per audio hour in a Microsoft answer, unverified because the official price page showed placeholders, and it would still need a lexicon for invented names ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5608069/pricing-and-usage-of-pronunciation-assessment-feat)). With the pick-one flow, a mispronounced name needs AJ once per name, by his own choice, and every take after that can be held to his pick automatically.

### The wrong word emphasized cannot be checked well enough to fail a take

Emphasis fails at both ends of a check. The best open detector is WhiStress (Interspeech 2025), a frozen whisper-small.en with a 7M-parameter stress head under a non-commercial licence. It reaches F1 0.96 on read speech but 0.689 zero-shot on expressive speech, with precision 0.573, so it marks extra words ([WhiStress](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1), [card](https://huggingface.co/slprl/WhiStress)). When the CAST benchmark checked it on TTS audio, it agreed with listeners at **kappa 0.32 while listeners agreed with each other at 0.36** ([CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)). It is as good as one more listener and no better, which is enough to rank takes and not enough to fail one. Audio-language models are worse: Gemini 2.5 Pro scores a stress-detection F1 of 50.5 ([StressTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22765v2)).

The target is soft too. Claude Haiku 4.5 picked the context-required stressed word 88.1% of the time on CAST's 226 constructed items, against 25 to 58% for small Qwen3 models, but CAST's own annotators agreed only 79% of the time pairwise ([CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)), and the EME-TTS authors note that several emphasis positions can be valid ([EME-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12015v1)). The fix side is weakest of all. On CAST, explicit emphasis markup lifted the best systems' hit rate only to about 52%, instructions and added context gave no clear gain, and getting both readings of a contrastive pair right was near zero for nearly every system ([CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10580)). Asterisks worked best for Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign in EmphTTS's pilot, at an emphasis F1 of 0.63 ([EmphTTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27599)), and Breeze's codec derives from Qwen3-TTS, but its text side is its own and its docs say nothing about asterisks or capitals ([breeze-tts](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts)). Capitals reach Breeze today and asterisks never do (`breeze.py:103-111`).

What is worth building is narrow. The screenplay pass can mark only lines with narrow or contrastive focus, such as corrections, "not X but Y", contrasted pronouns, echo questions and author italics in dialogue, naming the focus word and its competitors. On those lines alone, WhiStress, given the script text so it labels the intended tokens, can choose among takes that already pass the word check. The prosody researcher's arithmetic: if one take hits a context-required stress 30 to 42% of the time, roughly the spread CAST measured across systems, four independent takes give at least one hit 76 to 89% of the time, but seed independence is untested and the detector's noise makes some hits wrong. Two cheap tests come first: a scratch A/B of plain text against asterisks and capitals on one line and seed, scored by WhiStress, and a timing of WhiStress on the laptop CPU, since no speed is published. Rewriting a sentence so its default accent falls on the focus word, by end focus or a cleft, is the stronger text lever on paper and untested in practice. Shifting pitch and length on one word after the fact is a last resort, since WhiStress's own SSML-stressed training audio sounded robotic until its authors tuned it by hand ([WhiStress](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19103v1)).

## Better targets than the list: endings, long lines, drift and the text itself

Several defects reported for this model class are easier to catch than emphasis and at least as audible. The first is the ending. A Keeper trial scene dropped the end of two lines (`docs/casting.md:205`), XTTS users report text cut off abruptly ([XTTS-v2 discussion](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2/discussions/24)), and the Breeze tail collapse above faded a final sentence. Today an ending needs three dropped words to flag. The content researcher proposes counting a deletion of the line's last one or two script words as a failure on its own, which the existing alignment already locates. The second is length. The 58- and 64-word lines that lost about half their words match the skip class of the long-form literature, where failures grow with input length ([Taming long-form TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16989v1)), though that paper's shortest bin is under 500 words and nothing measures failure rates at 20 to 80 words. Breeze's maintainers advise keeping complete passages together under about 120 s for consistency ([breeze-tts issue 25](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/25)), so the two pressures pull opposite ways, and this report's reading of the data is to split only the outliers, such as the 72 Hundred Reigns lines over 60 words, at their strongest clause boundary at script time, before the first take. The third is the very short line. One-word lines gave 3 of the 27 suspects, all with empty transcripts, and Parakeet's card says it is not recommended for incomplete sentences ([Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2)). For lines of one to three words the content researcher proposes a cascade: accept an exact normalized match, accept a small character distance on a name, ask a recognizer from another family, and bound the speech span by syllable count times the voice's per-syllable range. Normalization gaps belong to the same fix: "Mister" against "Mr." accounted for 4 of the wrong words and a split "fiancée" for 2 more (`asr.py:184-301`).

The fourth is a voice drifting from itself. Breeze segments with the same reference and seed differed in timbre ([breeze-tts issue 25](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/25)), and speaker similarity over short windows exposes identity switches that whole-clip similarity averages away ([Taming long-form TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16989v1)). The field's measure is cosine similarity between speaker embeddings, where real human pairs score about 0.73 with WavLM-large ([WavTTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03455)), and sherpa-onnx ships CAM++, ERes2NetV2, WeSpeaker and TitaNet embedding models of about 26 to 221 MB ([sherpa-onnx speaker models](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/speaker-embedding-models/tree/main)). The signal researcher proposes comparing each take with the centroid of its voice's accepted takes rather than with the cast clip, flagging a take more than about 3 MAD below the voice's median or under an encoder-specific floor near 0.5 to 0.6 for ECAPA-class models, and retaking it, or using similarity as one score in a best-of-N pick but never the only one ([Llasa](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128v2)). Accent is the limit. A 2026 study found that the accent gap listeners hear between clones vanishes in several speaker-embedding spaces once each speaker's own variability is accounted for ([Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01562)), so the French accent AJ kept hearing on Argas Septic across seven casts (`docs/casting.md:41-67`) is out of reach of every check in this report. A whispered voice drifting back to full voice on about half its lines (`docs/casting.md:24`) is in reach in principle, by low loudness plus a voicing measure from a pitch tracker, as the researchers suggest, though nothing validates that on TTS.

The fifth is the room and the codec. AJ heard Casval's new voice as if in a hard-walled room, and no measurement told a roomy clip from a dry one (`docs/casting.md:124-144`). Brouhaha estimates per-frame SNR and C50, a clarity measure of reverberation, from one channel ([Brouhaha](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/brouhaha)), so it could score cast candidates and flag takes whose C50 departs from their voice's median, though the model is gated and needs pyannote. For codec glitches no mature detector exists. Because Breeze's codec works in 80 ms frames, the signal researcher expects a buzzy stretch to show as a run of 80 ms frames with abnormal spectral flatness, unlike the sub-millisecond clicks the join repairs, which nothing has validated. The frame-level quality model from Paderborn (ICASSP 2026) flagged segments of real voice clones that were mostly failed synthesis, low SNR, clipping or buzzing, but its recall on PartialSpoof was 0.404 ([frame-level SQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21886v1)), and its WavLM Large encoder makes it a Mac or between-renders tool. MOS predictors such as DNSMOS and Distill-MOS run cheaply on a CPU, but given their utterance-level accuracy they serve only as outlier scores against a voice's own median ([DNSMOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01763), [Distill-MOS](https://github.com/microsoft/Distill-MOS)).

The last group sits outside the audio. A retaken take is a pickup, and the Amazon patent accepts a re-recorded section only when its volume and tone match the audio around it ([US10922484](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10922484)), which maps to comparing a retake's level and pitch with its neighbours. The text a voice receives carries defects no take check fixes. Breeze normalizes nothing, so digits, symbols, Roman numerals and abbreviations reach it raw, and EmergentTTS-Eval found that kind of complex pronunciation a systematic failure of most open models, one that LLM text normalization significantly improves ([EmergentTTS-Eval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23009)). A lint before rendering that sends such a line back to the screenplay pass is an automatic fix with no audio cost. The screenplay pass can also drop or repeat a sentence of the chapter while every take passes, since its only guard is a word ratio of 0.9 to 1.2 (`screenplay.py:42-64`). A word diff of the script against the chapter, as Orion QC diffs audio against the manuscript ([Orion QC](https://tech.bertelsmann.com/en/blog/articles/orion-qc)), would flag dropped spans for a re-run, a check DECISIONS lists as undecided (`DECISIONS.md:210`). Speaker attribution can be cross-checked the same way: ModernBookNLP reports 94.5% quote-attribution accuracy on 22 novels of the 19th and 20th centuries ([ModernBookNLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02359)), untested on web-serial fantasy.

Three things the sources offer are poor value here. An audio-language model as a per-take judge fails on exactly the case a direction check needs, a frightened line read calmly, because such models answer from the words ([VoxParadox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27772)), and cost is not the obstacle, since an hour of takes is about $0.12 of Gemini audio input by the prosody researcher's arithmetic ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Generative restorers such as Sidon, Resemble Enhance and VoiceFixer re-synthesize the voice and can drift identity or content in ways MOS predictors do not catch ([PASE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40562/44523)), so they are a last resort for a take that fails across seeds, never a pass over every chapter. Bandwidth extension from Breeze's 24 kHz adds detail above 12 kHz that road noise and a bedside volume mostly hide, by the signal researcher's inference. One small encoding change is worth making instead: the Opus bitrate is soundfile's unknown default (`fx.py:80-89`), and 32 kbps mono is rated essentially transparent for speech and suitable for audiobooks ([Hydrogenaudio](https://wiki.hydrogenaud.io/?title=Opus)), so pinning it removes an unknown.

## Build in three phases, starting with data already in hand

**Before the phases, take again what the check has already flagged.** Ten flagged takes sit in nine rendered chapters of The Hundred Reigns, and no render will replace them, as the section on chapter 33 shows. Letting the retake loop cover a saved take whose stored verdict is `flagged` is a change of a few lines, needs no new measurement, and fixes the one defect AJ has reported by time and place. It replaces chapter audio he may have finished. AJ said yes to it on 2026-11-12, at a lower priority than the rest of this work.

**First, wire what the pipeline already computes into the retake loop, and add the pronunciation pick.** Options 1 to 9 target the largest error classes in AJ's own data (long lines, dropped endings, short lines, normalization gaps, names) and the two listed defects with standard measures (silences and volume). The pronunciation pick needs only breeze-server and a page beside the Cast tab. It fits the import batch, which already runs each chapter's character pass and then a cast of new voices (`README.md:103`), files its first candidate so the render never waits, and turns AJ's one decision per name into a say-as entry. The measurements should run in the Parakeet subprocess before the join, so a finding costs one take and not a breeze-server restart. This report recommends a shadow period: record the new measurements in `take_check.json` for a run of chapters before they may trigger retakes, then set each threshold from the per-voice distribution (median and MAD, or interquartile fences) and from the labelled cases on record, which are the 17 flagged takes, Lorimor's quiet takes, the Keeper's clipped clip, and the Keeper scene's dropped endings. AJ cannot review flags on a screen, so his library's distributions have to stand in for a reviewer. False alarms then cost GPU seconds, not his attention. As arithmetic from the recorded speeds, if new rules sent 2% of a 200-take chapter (chapter 7 had 182) to retake, one try each would add about 11 s on the laptop and 70 s on the Mac. That price holds only if keep-if-closer never swaps in a worse take, so the ranking in `badness` should put word errors first, then name scores, then signal flags, then prosody rank, with the shortest passing take as the tie-break the way Chatterbox-TTS-Extended picks it ([Chatter.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petermg/Chatterbox-TTS-Extended/main/Chatter.py)), or the take closest to the voice's speaking rate as the systems researcher suggests. When the nine-generation budget runs out, the loop should keep the best take and log it, an end state that needs nobody.

**Second, add three small CPU models, one of which screens pronunciation candidates and holds takes to AJ's picks.** The VAD and the speaker embedding run through the sherpa-onnx already in both audio Pythons and need only model files. The phone recognizer fits the laptop more easily than the Mac: wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak is a PyTorch model, ZIPA's ONNX loader imports torch, and the laptop's embedded Python has torch while the Mac's venv lists none (`docs/machines.md:14-15`, `docs/machines.md:85`), so the Mac would need a trimmed ONNX loader or the scoring would run on the laptop. Rendering several seeds of each pronunciation candidate for that screening belongs in its own job, like a cast, so it never shares a card with a chapter render. Each model should be timed on both CPUs before it is trusted, because none of ZIPA, the embedding models or WhiStress has a published CPU speed on this hardware. For the cross-family pick on flagged lines, Qwen3-ASR is the second family the repo already knows, but it needs the laptop's GPU through ComfyUI and the Mac has no ComfyUI (`DECISIONS.md:46`, `CLAUDE.md`, Where things run), so on the laptop it fits after breeze-server stops, with a 2.0 s restart for any retake. Everywhere else a cloud recognizer run only on the flagged fraction would cost well under a cent per finished hour at about $0.15 to $0.26 per hour of audio ([AssemblyAI](https://assemblyai.com/blog/speech-to-text-api-pricing), [Deepgram via aggregator](https://convertaudiototext.com/blog/deepgram-nova-3-explained)). A Whisper-family second opinion is a poor fit for short takes, since Whisper large-v3 produced text for 40.3% of non-speech clips ([Barański et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11378)).

**Later, and only as rankers.** Options 15 to 18 need a schema field or a PyTorch model, and their evidence supports choosing among takes, not failing one. emotion2vec+ large over-calls on TTS audio (happy at 0.70 against 0.32 for listeners, sad at 0.61 against 0.82) ([EME-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12015v1)), so it serves only as a veto of a flat contradiction. ParaSpeechCLAP's best-of-10 pick raised situational style-tag recall from 69.2% to 74.3% with no naturalness loss in a preprint ([ParaSpeechCLAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28737v1)), a small, real gain at ten times the render cost. For pitch, SwiftF0 tracks 5 s of audio in 132.6 ms on a CPU against 1,420.6 ms for librosa's pYIN in its author's own benchmark ([SwiftF0](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18440v1)). Existing chapters are a separate decision: takes from earlier renders are never taken again by design (`DECISIONS.md:46`), so new checks can audit them through a `check-takes`-style job on the node that holds the takes. Fixing what they find takes more than a re-render, because a render reuses a saved take whatever its verdict (`breeze.py:677-685`), so the change described before the phases is what lets a re-render AJ chooses act on a finding.

## How far to trust these numbers

No figure in the sources was measured on Breeze TTS 2 output in English. The Breeze-specific evidence is GitHub issues: one 26-seed Mandarin sweep, in which a tail collapse and a misread each appeared in one seed, and user reports of segment-to-segment drift ([breeze-tts issue 14](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/14), [issue 25](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts/issues/25)), for a model whose weights came out on 2026-08-25. AJ's own measured data is narrow: 824 takes of four chapters in the retired copy, checked by Qwen3-ASR rather than the Parakeet that checks takes today, plus the live figure of 17 flagged in 7,602 from DECISIONS. The one bad take AJ named, The Hundred Reigns chapter 33 at 8:55 to 9:05, was examined after the rest of this report was written. It had been flagged and never taken again, and the count of ten such takes comes from the live library on 2026-11-12. The ranking of the nineteen options was written before that examination, which adds the step before the phases and does not reorder them. Several load-bearing 2026 sources are arXiv preprints with no venue named in the notes, among them CAST, EmphTTS, the long-form paper, AlignDPO, GRAFT, ParaSpeechCLAP, ModernBookNLP and the MFA 2026 comparison, while WhiStress (Interspeech 2025), ZIPA (ACL 2025), SpeechJudge (ICLR 2026), Yu and Kang and VoxParadox (ICML 2026), the frame-level SQA model (ICASSP 2026) and Yang et al. (IEEE SLT 2026) carry venues. say-it-right is days old and its accuracy is its author's own fit. Some figures rest on search snippets or secondary pages: ElevenLabs' Auto-Regenerate description, InstructTTSEval's agreement figures (left out of this report for that reason), StressTest's version-dependent scores, the Azure price and the Deepgram price. Seed-to-seed independence at Breeze's temperature of 0.9, which every best-of-N estimate here assumes, is untested. Every threshold a researcher proposed for audiogen, including the pause gaps, the loudness offsets and swing limits, the MAD and percentile cut-offs and the similarity floor, is a starting point to tune, not a published value.

## Conclusion

The five defects split along one line: whether the right answer can be written down before the voice speaks. Where the target is arithmetic, a pause against its punctuation or a level against its voice, or history, a name against its earlier occurrences, the fix can be automatic now, and the work is plumbing rather than research. Where the target is a human judgment (the focus word, the intended pronunciation, the acting), the leverage is to move that judgment upstream into the Claude passes, which can emit a focus word, an intended IPA, a heteronym sense or a split direction as text. That turns a soft audio judgment into a narrow comparison against a stated target. For names it leaves AJ the one decision he has said he wants, a pick per name made the way he picks a voice, and leaves the machine the part he cannot do from the driver's seat, holding every later take to that pick. Emphasis shows the limit of the approach: even with a stated target, today's detector is only as reliable as one more listener, so it can steer a choice and never decide one.

The other lesson is about cost. Tools built for a human proofer must keep false alarms rare, because each costs a person's time. In audiogen a false alarm costs a few GPU seconds, so the pipeline can afford to be stricter than any tool surveyed, provided it measures before it acts, selects with a verifier from a different recognizer family than the one that judges, and ends every line in a defined state when the budget runs out. The single most valuable change is not a model. It is moving measurements the pipeline already makes into the one moment when a retake is cheap.
