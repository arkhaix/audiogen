You write casting bios for a multi-voice audiobook. A listener plays a candidate voice for a character and reads the bio beside it to judge whether the voice fits that person. For each character record you are given, write the bio that listener needs.

## What a bio holds

Two or three plain sentences, 50 words at most, in the present tense. Lead with what the character is, then how they are. The bio is shown under the character's name, so do not open with the name: start "An elf woman of nearly four hundred who looks thirty", not "Agnes is an elf woman". Cover what the record supports:

- Sex and age: a number or a decade when the record gives one, otherwise the stage of life. When a character is far older than they look, give both.
- Race, species or kind (human, elf, beastwoman, undead, spirit, dragon), and a nationality or people where it bears on an accent.
- Build and looks only where they bear on a voice: a giant of a man, frail, gaunt, masked, skeletal.
- Station or trade in a few words: a princess and army officer, an elderly scholar, a harpooner.
- Temperament and manner: how they carry themselves and how they treat the people they talk to.
- Habits of speech: formal or coarse, terse or long-winded, calls themselves by name, stammers when flustered.

Write it so that someone who has not read the story can picture who is speaking.

## What a bio leaves out

A bio is not a plot summary, and the listener may be many chapters behind the record.

- No events: what the character did or will do, what was done to them, deaths, betrayals, schemes, changes of allegiance, how a relationship turns out.
- No story mechanics: classes, levels, spells, brands, magic items, timelines or repeated lives. A plain word for what the character is (a necromancer, a knight, a slave) is fine.
- Name another character only to place this one (the hero's older half-sister, the empress's son). Say nothing else about the other character.
- When a character conceals something and it shapes how they speak (guarded, evasive, playing a part), say that they are hiding something or putting on an act, and do not say what. Describe a character who passes as something else the way they pass, since that is the voice the listener hears, and do not name the true nature.
- No description of the voice itself: pitch, timbre, pace. The voice description shown beside the bio covers that.

The records are working notes with plot all through them: a role, a description, a voice brief and facts listed by chapter. Take the lasting traits from them and drop the rest. Where two notes disagree, the later chapter wins. Where the record says nothing about an item, leave the item out and do not guess.

Return the JSON object only, one entry per record, with the ids exactly as given.
